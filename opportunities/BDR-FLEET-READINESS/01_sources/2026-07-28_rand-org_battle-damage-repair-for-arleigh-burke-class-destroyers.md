## Summary

RAND National Security Research Division report on a tabletop exercise run 13–14 August 2025 with
United States and allied Indo-Pacific participants, examining what it would take to salvage and
repair battle-damaged Arleigh Burke–class destroyers in a Pacific conflict. Sponsored by the Office
of the Assistant Secretary of War for Sustainment's Product Support office.

This is the most directly on-point public source in the opportunity. Its top finding is ambiguous
command and control, and two of its five Navy-facing recommendations are training and exercise
recommendations. It reached the same problem the operator's SWRMC evidence shows the Navy acting on.

## Verbatim: scope and method

> The Arleigh Burke–class guided missile destroyer, commonly known as a DDG-51, is the primary
> surface combatant vessel of the U.S. Navy. These ships—designed to operate in contested
> environments—are likely to sustain widespread battle damage from diverse attacks should a conflict
> develop in the Indo-Pacific region. The prevalent nature of this damage would give rise to the
> greatest demand for ship repair that the Navy has experienced since World War II.

> To evaluate requirements for and potential issues with a significant demand for salvaging and
> repairing battle-damaged destroyers, the authors conducted a tabletop exercise on August 13–14,
> 2025, with participants from the United States and allied nations in the Indo-Pacific. The
> participants were split into four groups, and each group represented U.S. and allied stakeholders
> who would be involved in the repairs.

> The exercise involved four vignettes in which ships, including DDG-51s, were damaged and required
> repair. The exercise focused on the capabilities required to salvage and repair the ships, the
> implied command-and-control (C2) arrangements, partner nations' roles, and key organic
> capabilities that the U.S. Navy could deploy and develop.

## Verbatim: key takeaways

> Existing U.S. Navy systems for battle-damage repair are burdened by outdated peacetime frameworks
> and ambiguous C2 structures during conflict.

> Presumptive expectations concerning even close allies' abilities and willingness to support
> extensive repairs might be unrealistic. Participants from allied nations were clear in their
> support of alliance commitments, but they noted that most repair facilities are in significantly
> vulnerable areas. It is not clear whether the United States could rely on partners without formal
> alliance ties to support U.S. actions beyond brief stops for voyage repairs.

> Although the Navy has organic repair capabilities, these capabilities are sufficient only for
> limited repairs to combat systems and repairs that allow ships to transit back to the United
> States.

> Poor availability of necessary parts might cause significant delays, which could occur even if a
> host nation has agreed to provide more-substantive repair support.

## Verbatim: recommendations to the U.S. Navy

> The U.S. Navy should institutionalize C2 by clarifying authority, streamlining communications, and
> defining approval chains during crisis and conflict.

> The U.S. Navy should expand organic repair capabilities and ensure that all deployed personnel are
> familiar with rapidly deploying those capabilities.

> The U.S. Navy should negotiate detailed support agreements with regional partners and exercise
> repair procedures without access to key ports.

> The U.S. Navy should reconstitute critical parts and a skilled workforce to prioritize operational
> flexibility over peacetime efficiency.

> The U.S. Navy should anchor exercises to strategic objectives, prioritizing assured force
> regeneration, tactical resilience, and alliance interoperability.

## Verbatim: recommendations to allies and partners (selected)

> Allies and partners should, for future wargames, include realistic scenarios and emphasize a mutual
> understanding of C2, information-sharing, and allied support during high-threat situations.

> Allies and partners should codevelop host-nation resilience and continuity plans that focus on port
> and repair facilities, distributed repair nodes, and partner-specific continuity-of-operations
> plans.

> Allies and partners should advance multinational pre-positioning of repair packages with clear
> protocols for accelerated access during a crisis to avoid diplomatic bottlenecks.

## Why this matters to the opportunity

**Assessment.** Three alignments, in descending order of strength.

The first recommendation is command and control: clarify authority, streamline communications, define
approval chains. The capture brief argues the opportunity is an undefined coordination seam between
SWRMC and SRF-JRMC on the container mission. That was an inference drawn from what Robinson did not
say in his keynote. RAND states it directly as the leading problem, from an exercise with allied
participants, in a peer-reviewed and federally sponsored report.

The second recommendation pairs capability with familiarity: expand organic repair capability **and**
ensure deployed personnel are familiar with rapidly deploying it. SWRMC has built the capability, in
the form of roughly 50 pre-staged expeditionary maintenance containers. The familiarity half is the
product this opportunity proposes. RAND recommends both halves in one sentence and the Navy is
visibly executing only the first.

The fifth recommendation, and the allied recommendation on future wargames, are both exercise
recommendations. RAND's own method was a two-day tabletop exercise. The most authoritative treatment
of this problem used the technique the product sells, once, to discover that the Navy is not ready.
Discovery is not rehearsal, and the commands that would carry out the mission were not the ones in
the room.

**Assessment, funding.** The sponsor is the Office of the Assistant Secretary of War for
Sustainment's Product Support office. Given that the container mission itself is unfunded, the study
sponsor is a more plausible payer lead than the fleet commands and should be worked in Phase 1.

**Speculation, flagged.** Whether the sponsor has money for training as opposed to analysis is
unknown and nothing here supports an inference either way.
