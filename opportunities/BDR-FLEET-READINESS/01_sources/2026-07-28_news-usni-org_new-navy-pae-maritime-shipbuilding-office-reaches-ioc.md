## Facts of record

- PAE Maritime reached initial operating capability per a 16 July 2026 memo. Led by Chris Miller.
- It runs five deputy portfolios. One is **Ship Readiness**, led by Rear Adm. Andy Biehn with
  civilian Lisa Bonacic-Doric, covering maintenance for small combatants and combatants, the
  Arleigh Burke–class DDG 2.0 modification, **training systems**, and international support.
- **PAE Industrial Operations** is led by Vice Adm. Jim Downey, who also heads NAVSEA, and oversees
  maintenance and the public shipyards.
- Other PAEs stood up: Undersea, Strategic Systems Programs, Marine Corps.

## Why this matters to the opportunity

**Assessment. The ambiguity this brief is built on runs higher than the theater.** Section 2.7
records that no public source establishes who owns the Pacific container mission. This source shows
why that is not an oversight. The organization that would hold industrial ownership of battle damage
repair is being taken apart and rebuilt right now, roughly 70 percent of it by workforce and
function, and a comprehensive study is running to decide what the systems commands become. RAND
found command and control ambiguous in a wartime exercise. The peacetime organization above it is
also unsettled, and unsettled on the record.

**Assessment. The brief's existing acquisition target is going stale.** Earlier versions name NAVSEA
04 as a systems-command contracting authority. Maintenance and the public shipyards now sit under
PAE Industrial Operations. NAVSEA continues in engineering, technical expertise and infrastructure,
so NAVSEA 05 remains the right technical-authority reference for the data-bridge question, but
NAVSEA 04 should no longer be presented as the buying authority without qualification.

**Assessment, and it is the most actionable item here.** PAE Maritime's Ship Readiness portfolio
covers maintenance for combatants and training systems in the same portfolio. That is the
intersection this product sits in. It is a named organization with named leadership and it is new,
which means its processes and vehicles are being set now.

**Assessment on risk.** Selling into an organization mid-reorganization is hard in a specific way.
Reporting lines move, budgets are re-pointed, and the office briefed today may not hold the decision
tomorrow. Against that, new organizations have to define how they buy, and a defined product
arriving while those decisions are being made can land better than one arriving after they harden.
Both belong in the brief; neither should be asserted as the dominant effect.

**Speculation, flagged.** Whether the systems-command study changes battle-damage-repair ownership
specifically is unknown. Nothing here says the study addresses it.
