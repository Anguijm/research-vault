# Play 1 — Government-side assurance of the foreign-yard USN ship-repair enterprise

Single-page operator reference. This is an **ideation-stage** play and is largely
**Assessment / Speculation** — a forward pursuit thesis, not a sourced procurement.
The Hanwha track's FACT base is in `00_research-file.md` §3–§6; this card is the
"what CACI could do about it" layer. Status stays *ideation* until the gating
questions below are answered.

## The one-line thesis

As the U.S. Navy deliberately pushes ship work into foreign (Korean) yards, position
CACI as the **government's independent assurance agent** — quality assurance,
technical oversight, and security / supply-chain-risk assurance of work performed at
yards the government does not control — selling to the **Navy**, never to the yards.

## What the customer wants

> "USNS Wally Schirra successfully completed a dry-docking Regular Overhaul (ROH)
> period at Hanwha Ocean … This ROH was a significant building block for realizing
> the Navy's vision to broaden the scope of ship maintenance and repair work in the
> Indo-Pacific region."
> — MSC 2025 Annual Report [s.2026-06-07-msc-2025-annual-report-in-rev]

> "Maintenance in Theater reduces downtime and costs, while enhancing operational
> readiness." — Rear Adm. Neil Koprowski, Commander U.S. Naval Forces Korea
> [s.2026-06-07-navy-mil-wally-schirra-roh]

**Translation:** the Navy has *stated intent* to distribute more ship maintenance to
in-theater and foreign yards (Hanwha, and per §5 also HD Hyundai and Samsung+Vigor).
Work it does not directly control still has to meet Navy quality, configuration,
technical-data, security, and supply-chain standards. That assurance burden grows
with the trend — and it is government-side work, distinct from the yards' steel/labor.

## The play

**Headline:** Sell the Navy (NAVSUP FLC Yokosuka / MSC N7 / MSFSC / NAVSEA) a standing
**foreign-yard assurance** capability — QA, technical/configuration oversight,
technical-data management, and security/SCRM assurance of USN ship availabilities
executed at Korean yards — extending CACI's existing MSC shipyard-QA incumbency into
the new in-theater enterprise.

**Constraining principles:**

1. **Government-side only. Never sell to Hanwha or any yard.** This is what keeps CACI
   on the clean side of the Organizational Conflict of Interest (OCI) line and lets it
   be the *impartial* assurance agent over **all** the foreign yards (Hanwha, HD
   Hyundai, Samsung+Vigor). The OCI constraint becomes the positioning. See
   `_meta/oci-primer.md`.
2. **Anchor on incumbency, not novelty.** Frame as growth of the proven MSC N7 shipyard-
   QA work, not a new unproven line.
3. **No forward-presence overclaim.** CACI is not yet performing in Korea; pitch the
   capability + vehicle + readiness to extend, not a footprint it lacks.

## TAM — sized 2026-06-10 from observed award data (and honest about the bet)

This sizes two layers: the Korea-yard USN maintenance/repair/overhaul (MRO) market
(the pool), and CACI's government-side assurance slice (the actual play). Anchors
are from research file §3.1 (award data) and §5 (competitive field).

**The observed market (FACT — USAspending).** Korea-yard USN auxiliary repair
awards visible in our corpus total **~$103M across ~8 availabilities in ~18 months**
(six at Hanwha, two at HD Hyundai) — a current run-rate on the order of
**~$60–90M/yr** (Assessment; likely an undercount, bounded by the 30-source corpus).
It grew from zero before mid-2024, so the demand is real, not hype.

**Per-availability value — correction to the old anchor.** The earlier "~$26M per
regular overhaul (ROH)" figure is the high end — Hanwha's major dry-dock ROHs only.
HD Hyundai's ROHs came in at $7–8.5M; the blended ROH average is **~$19M**, and
across all availabilities (including mid-term and voyage repairs) **~$13M**. Plan on
~$13–19M per availability, not $26M.

**What caps the pool (Assessment).** Every observed availability is a USNS
*auxiliary* — no surface combatants, no USS (and the Fleet Logistics Center Yokosuka
Industry Day deck confirms USNS needs no Master Ship Repair Agreement while USS
still does). So the addressable fleet is Military Sealift Command's Pacific Combat
Logistics Force, not "the Navy's ships"; Korea competes for each availability against
Japan, Singapore, and others in full-and-open, lowest-price-technically-acceptable
competition; and only a handful of in-theater auxiliaries cycle through depot work
per year. Realistic market ceiling, auxiliaries only: **~$100–200M/yr across all
Korean yards** — above today's run-rate, but not an order of magnitude above it.

**CACI's slice — the number that actually matters (Assessment).** The play is
government-side assurance (quality assurance, technical oversight, security/supply-
chain-risk management), historically a low single-digit percent of availability
value (soft assumption, ~2–8%):

| Layer | Range | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Korea-yard USN MRO market — today | ~$60–90M/yr | medium (FACT-derived) |
| Korea-yard USN MRO market — realistic ceiling (auxiliaries) | ~$100–200M/yr | low |
| CACI government-side assurance slice — realistic, today | ~$1.3–7M/yr | low |
| CACI slice — matured ceiling / over a 5-yr vehicle | ~$3–16M/yr; ~$15–80M total | low |

**Be honest about the bet.** The realistic central case is **a few million dollars a
year**. Against CACI's existing $125.6M MSC machinery/structural-maintenance task
order (FA807522F0054), Play 1 is an **incumbency extension and cheap positioning in a
fast-growing niche — not a franchise worth a dedicated capture**. Two soft numbers
drive everything and compound: the assurance percentage, and whether the Navy
*contracts* this at all versus keeping it organic (the play's top gate, still
unanswered). The one thing that breaks the ceiling materially upward is **USS surface
combatants moving to Korean yards** — which is not happening today (Speculation),
faces real security and sovereignty barriers, but is where the government-side
oversight value would multiply. So Play 1's upside is a bet on combatant policy, not
on auxiliary volume. Pursue it for what it is: a low-cost incumbency play with
optionality on a policy shift, not a revenue line to staff up for.

| Unsolicited — no stated requirement yet | Accept long sell; lead with a concept paper, not a bid |
