## 1. Working summary (analyst view)

This is an exploratory study, not yet a pursuit. Hanwha Ocean is a South Korean
shipbuilder that has become the first Korean yard to perform U.S. Navy
maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO) and has bought and is recapitalizing a
U.S. yard (Philly Shipyard) for naval construction. The core question is whether
there is an opportunity here for CACI, and of what kind — selling capability to
Hanwha, teaming with Hanwha, or treating Hanwha as landscape intel that informs
the operator's existing Pacific ship-repair work. The working hypothesis is
deliberately left open (see §7); this first source pass is about establishing
what is factually happening, not yet judging the fit.

A note on the first source pass (2026-06-07): the CACI intersection is **not yet
evidenced by any source**. Nothing ingested ties Hanwha Ocean's activity to a
CACI capability area. Per the vault's named-entity discipline, no such link is
asserted here until a source surfaces one.

## 3. Demand signal — what is actually happening

Lead: in roughly 18 months Hanwha Ocean went from no U.S. Navy work to an MSRA,
multiple auxiliary MRO availabilities (now confirmed in federal award data with
exact dollar values), a U.S. shipyard acquisition, a $5B recapitalization pledge,
and a first U.S. Navy construction-side subcontract.

### 3.1 The MRO line (Korea-based, on USNS auxiliaries)

- **FACT:** Hanwha Ocean signed a Master Ship Repair Agreement (MSRA) with the U.S. Navy's Naval Supply Systems Command (NAVSUP) on 22 July 2024, after a ~7-month application, granting 5-year eligibility to bid U.S. Navy MRO [s.2026-06-07-hanwha-ocean-becomes-first-sou] [s.2026-06-07-hanwha-ocean-secures-msra-cert].
- **FACT:** USNS *Wally Schirra* (T-AKE 8) underwent a seven-month regular overhaul (ROH) at Hanwha Ocean and redelivered 12 March 2025 — the first time a ROK yard bid on and won an ROH of this scale for an MSC vessel. Scope: dry docking, 300+ work items, hull corrosion, and a full rudder replacement that Hanwha engineers reverse-engineered when no blueprints existed [s.2026-06-07-navy-mil-wally-schirra-roh] [s.2026-06-07-hanwha-ocean-completes-wally-s].
- **FACT (federal award data):** The auxiliary MRO availabilities are visible in USAspending with exact obligations and the Navy as the awarding agency:
  - Wally Schirra ROH — PIID N4034524F0559 — $26,024,973 — 28 Aug 2024 to 15 Mar 2025 [s.2026-06-07-usns-wally-schirra-roh-cat-a].
  - Yukon ROH — PIID N6264925FL025 — $27,183,047 — 8 Nov 2024 to 25 Jul 2025 [s.2026-06-07-usns-yukon-roh-cat-a].
  - Charles Drew ROH — PIID N6264925CL015 — $26,027,460 — 1 Jul 2025 to 28 Feb 2026 [s.2026-06-07-usns-charles-drew-roh].
  - Wally Schirra mid-term availability (MTA) — PIID N6264925CLA04 — $7,317,871 — 26 Sep 2025 to 8 Feb 2026 [s.2026-06-07-usns-wally-schirra-t-ake-8-mid].
  - USNS Richard E. Byrd (T-AKE 4) voyage repair at Chinhae, South Korea — PIID N6264926FK006 — $360,010 — Feb 2026 [s.2026-06-07-usns-richard-e-byrd-t-ake-4-vo].
  - "Emergent repair services" — PIID N6264925FR036 — $238,850 — Jul–Nov 2025 [s.2026-06-07-emergent-repair-services].
- **Assessment:** The three big ROHs cluster tightly around $26–27M each — a usable benchmark for the per-availability value of a Lewis-and-Clark-class/oiler ROH in Korea. The Byrd voyage repair and the emergent-repair award show the work is broadening beyond the headline ROHs into smaller in-theater availabilities — i.e., a recurring book of business, not three one-offs.
- **FACT (contracting chain, now confirmed):** The awarding/contracting office on these awards — both the N40345 and N62649 PIID families — is **NAVSUP Fleet Logistics Center Yokosuka** (confirmed via USAspending award detail). The funding office is **Military Sealift Fleet Support Command (MSFSC)**. So FLC Yokosuka contracts the work and MSC/MSFSC funds it — the same contracting world as the operator's SRF-JRMC/Japan footprint.

### 3.2 The construction line (Philly Shipyard, U.S.-based)

- **FACT:** Hanwha acquired Philly Shipyard for ~$100M (CFIUS approval Sept 2024; close around Dec 2024) and renamed it Hanwha Philly Shipyard [s.2026-06-07-hanwha-acquires-philly-shipyar] [s.2026-06-07-usni-5b-philly].
- **FACT:** Hanwha pledged ~$5B in capex to expand the yard (added docks/quays, a possible new block-assembly facility), aiming to lift output from under two vessels/year toward up to 20; near-term LNG carriers and naval modules/blocks, longer-term naval vessels [s.2026-06-07-hanwha-announces-5-billion-phi] [s.2026-06-07-usni-5b-philly].
- **FACT:** The $5B was disclosed 26 Aug 2025, one day after the ROK president met President Trump; Hanwha frames it inside a $150B South Korea pledge to U.S. shipbuilding under a U.S.–Korea trade agreement [s.2026-06-07-usni-5b-philly].
- **FACT:** CEO David Kim said the yard is "already in discussions with the Navy [for] different opportunities" and hopes to build ~10 ships/year within a decade [s.2026-06-07-usni-5b-philly].
- **FACT:** Hanwha Defense USA and Hanwha Philly Shipyard received a first U.S. Navy subcontract tied to the Next Generation Logistics Ship (NGLS) — market survey and concept design/refinement [s.2026-06-07-hanwha-defense-usa-wins-u-s-na].

### 3.3 Engagement mechanism / who buys

- **FACT:** The MRO line is bought through NAVSUP (MSRA holder) and run operationally through Military Sealift Command — MSC Office-Korea, MSC Far East, supporting U.S. 7th Fleet [s.2026-06-07-navy-mil-wally-schirra-roh] [s.2026-06-07-hanwha-ocean-becomes-first-sou].
- **FACT:** The standing industry-engagement vehicle is the MSC "Korea and Singapore Ship Repair Industry Day," run as **MSC-FLCY Industry Day** (FLCY = Fleet Logistics Center Yokosuka), with an Annual Submittals Program as the formal interest-registration route [s.2026-06-07-msc-flcy-industry-day] [s.2026-06-07-flcy-industry-day-2025-acquisi].
- **FACT (how MSC actually buys this — from the FLCY Industry Day deck):** all MSC Pacific ship-repair requirements are now competed **full and open**, posted on **SAM.gov and AsiaNeco**, awarded **Firm-Fixed-Price** on a **Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA)** basis; and **USNS platforms no longer require an MSRA/ABR** (only USS platforms do) [s.2026-06-07-flcy-industry-day-2025-acquisi]. The maintenance cadence is structured as Voyage Repair / Mid-Term Availability / Regular Overhaul / Service Order, matching the award types seen in §3.1.
- **FACT (MSC's own strategic intent — the strongest demand signal):** MSC's 2025 annual report calls the Wally Schirra overhaul at Hanwha "a significant building block for realizing the Navy's vision to broaden the scope of ship maintenance and repair work in the Indo-Pacific region" [s.2026-06-07-msc-2025-annual-report-in-rev]. This frames the Korea MRO as durable strategic intent, not a one-off, and is corroborated by SECNAV Carlos Del Toro's "game-changing milestone in our new Maritime Statecraft" (now verified verbatim — see `02_quotes.md`) [s.2026-06-07-hanwha-ocean-becomes-first-sou].
- **Assessment:** The buyer for the MRO line is the U.S. Navy (NAVSUP FLC Yokosuka / MSC / MSFSC), not Hanwha — so on the maintenance side Hanwha is the *executing yard*, not the customer. The LPTA/FFP structure means the MRO contracting itself is a price-driven yard competition with no professional-services insertion point. This is decisive for where a CACI angle can and cannot live (see §6).

## 4. Customer landscape

The relevant DoD customers, by line of business: for MRO, **NAVSUP FLC Yokosuka**
(contracting), **Military Sealift Fleet Support Command / MSFSC** (funding), and
**Military Sealift Command** (MSC Office-Korea / MSC Far East, operational),
serving **7th Fleet**; for construction, the **Navy** shipbuilding enterprise
(NGLS program office and, prospectively, larger naval construction). Hanwha sits
on the *supplier* side of all of these. Named Navy people surfaced so far: Rear
Adm. Neil Koprowski (Commander, U.S. Naval Forces Korea) and Cmdr. Patrick J.
Moore (CO, MSC Office-Korea) — see `02_quotes.md`.

The strategically important point (developed in §6): **MSC is also a CACI
customer.** The same MSC whose availabilities are moving to Korean yards already
buys machinery/structural-maintenance engineering, shipyard QA, condition-based
maintenance, worldwide logistics, and business systems from CACI. So Hanwha and
CACI sit on the same side of the table (both serve the Navy), not across from
each other.

## 5. Competitive / context landscape — the MASGA / Korea-yard trend

Lead: Hanwha is the lead actor but not the only one — a cluster of Korean yards
is entering U.S. Navy MRO and construction simultaneously under the same policy
umbrella, which is the "trend" the operator asked to research alongside the firm.

- **FACT:** The activity sits under "MASGA" (Make American Shipbuilding Great Again), a U.S.–Korea shipbuilding cooperation framework backed by a ~$150B Korea investment pledge tied to a U.S.–Korea trade agreement [s.2026-06-07-south-korean-manufacturers-out] [s.2026-06-07-usni-5b-philly].
- **FACT (federal award data — HD Hyundai is doing the same MRO work):** HD Hyundai Heavy Industries won USN auxiliary ROHs on the same MSC/Navy contracting line: USNS Alan Shepard (T-AKE 3) ROH — PIID N6264925CL021 — $8,535,342 — Aug–Dec 2025 [s.2026-06-07-usns-alan-shepard-t-ake-3-roh]; and USNS Cesar Chavez ROH 2026 — PIID N6264926CL003 — $7,408,203 — Dec 2025–Mar 2026 [s.2026-06-07-usns-cesar-chavez-regular-over].
- **FACT (a third pairing):** Samsung Heavy Industries is teaming with the U.S. firm Vigor Marine Group to bring forward-deployed MRO capacity to the Indo-Pacific, explicitly pitched to the U.S. Navy and MSC [s.2026-06-07-usni-5b-philly].
- **FACT:** HD Hyundai and HJ Shipbuilding are also named as entering U.S. Navy MRO and/or construction roles [s.2026-06-07-south-korean-manufacturers-out].
- **FACT (the full competitive field):** The MSC-FLCY 2025 Industry Day attendee roster shows the actual field of yards pursuing this work across the AOR — Korea (Hanwha Ocean, HD Hyundai, HJ Shipbuilding, Sunjin Entech, Dongil, Orient, Jonghap, DSSB, Khan Offshore), Japan (Sasebo/Namura, Sumitomo, Mitsubishi, Shinko), Singapore (Seatrium, ST Engineering Marine, Goltens, MAN, others), India (Cochin Shipyard), Australia (Austal, Griffin), Thailand (Unithai), Philippines (Herma), and Guam yards [s.2026-06-07-industry-day-2025-attendees]. As of 27 Jan 2025 the AOR had 16 MSRA and 33 ABR holders; South Korea held 3 MSRA + 3 ABR [s.2026-06-07-flcy-industry-day-2025-acquisi].
- **Assessment:** The "market" is a Navy-led push to stand up in-theater MRO capacity across many competing yards (LPTA price competition), plus onshore Korean investment in U.S. construction. The competitive field is entirely shipyards/marine-trade firms — no professional-services integrators — confirming the *yard* layer is not a CACI market. The CACI angle therefore lives on the **government side** of this program, where it turns out CACI is already a sourced incumbent (see §6).
- Competitor-to-CACI lens: confirmed least likely; the field is yards, and no source frames any of them as a CACI competitor.

## 6. Our fit — where the CACI capability intersects

Lead: the intersection is now **sourced, not speculative**. CACI is already a
Military Sealift Command prime in the government-side engineering/QA/logistics
layer that sits directly above the yard work — and MSC is the very customer
moving ship availabilities into Korean yards. CACI and Hanwha are on the same
side of the table.

### 6.1 The sourced bridge (FACT)

- **FACT:** CACI holds a Military Sealift Command task order (announced 29 Sep 2022, $174M; USAspending PIID FA807522F0054, ~$125.6M obligated, via a DoD Information Analysis Center multiple-award vehicle) to optimize MSC's **machinery and structural maintenance program** — RAMS engineering, life-cycle engineering RDT&E, **quality-assurance planning and analysis**, and **Condition-Based Maintenance (CBM)** — with **"worldwide deployments to shipyards during MSC ship availabilities"** [s.2026-06-07-caci-msc-fleet-readiness-to] [s.2026-06-07-design-maintenance-quality-ext].
- **FACT (corroborating CACI-MSC footprint, now primary-sourced):** Beyond the maintenance-engineering task order, CACI is a long-standing MSC prime in two other lines: **MSC Integrated Business Systems** (PIID N3220519F1044, ~$165M obligated, issued by MSC's own contracting office N32205 and riding the CIO-SP3 GWAC — the 2019 anchor, with FY25 follow-ons) [s.2026-06-07-n102a-staley-msc-integrated-bu]; and **MSC Worldwide Logistics Services** (PIID N3220524C4115, the 2024 FFP award, ~$20M obligated to date) [s.2026-06-07-n102d-n4-v-principe-worldwide-]. So CACI sits across MSC's maintenance-engineering, business-systems, and logistics layers — a deep incumbent on the buyer side of the Korea MRO trend.
- **Assessment:** Put together with §3–§5, the thesis is a **pull-through, not a new market entry**: MSC is pushing more availabilities into Korean yards (Hanwha, HD Hyundai, …); CACI's existing MSC contracts already put its engineers/QA into shipyards *during MSC availabilities*; therefore the growth in Korea-based MRO is latent growth in demand for the government-side engineering/QA/CBM work CACI already does for MSC. The buyer is the Navy (MSC/MSFSC/NAVSUP FLC Yokosuka), not the CFIUS-sensitive foreign yard.

### 6.2 Secondary / lower-confidence threads (still Speculation)

- **Engineering-data / reverse-engineering / digital-twin.** The Wally Schirra rudder was reverse-engineered with no blueprints; in-theater MRO of aging auxiliaries will keep generating "no-tech-data" problems — a possible CACI engineering/CBM adjacency. *To test against CACI's actual portfolio.*
- **Shipyard digital transformation at Hanwha Philly Shipyard** (production IT/ERP/MES). Selling to a foreign-owned commercial yard with CFIUS/security overlays — harder, not obviously CACI core. Lower priority.
- **Security/IT/compliance overlay for foreign yards touching USN ships/data.** Plausible adjacency; no source indicates a procurement. Lowest confidence.

### 6.3 Existing footprint

The most concrete thread to pull is the overlap between CACI's MSC
maintenance-engineering work and the operator's own **NAVSUP FLC Yokosuka /
SRF-JRMC** world (see [[project_operator_team_at_srf_jrmc]]). The contracting
office that buys the Korea MRO (FLC Yokosuka) is the operator's adjacent
contracting environment.

## 7. Working hypothesis

**Framing (operator-set 2026-06-07):** subject = **Hanwha Ocean, the firm**;
context = the **MASGA / Korea-yard MRO trend** (§5); workstream = **where a CACI
capability intersects** (§6).

**Candidate hypothesis (Assessment — first one the evidence supports):** *The
Navy's shift of MSC auxiliary availabilities into Korean yards (Hanwha-led) is a
pull-through opportunity for CACI's existing MSC machinery/structural-maintenance
engineering, shipyard-QA, and condition-based-maintenance work — i.e., CACI's
role is government-side technical support to the in-theater MRO enterprise, not
selling to or teaming with the yards.* This is now a sourced thesis rather than a
guess, but it was **not yet validated** as of the candidate-hypothesis stage.

**Validation result (2026-06-07), then CORRECTED by operator input — it is the
operator's own task order, and CACI already fields the deploying contingent.** The
USAspending read (FA807522F0054 booked to Norfolk; zero Pacific ship-maintenance
place-of-performance) first looked like "white space / no CACI presence." The
operator corrected the record: **FA807522F0054 is the operator-team's own task
order (MSC N7 Engineering Directorate)**, and that single TO spans two
non-overlapping contingents — the operator's team (USS repair at SRF-JRMC) and a
**separate worldwide contingent that performs USNS shipyard-QA / maintenance-
engineering at MSC ship availabilities.** So CACI is NOT absent: the contractor
that would provide government-side QA/engineering at the Korea/Hanwha USNS
availabilities is CACI's USNS contingent **on the operator's own contract**. The
Norfolk place-of-performance is a contracting-HQ artifact, not the real footprint.

Corrected thesis: this is an **incumbency-extension** play, not white space. CACI
already holds the MSC N7 maintenance-engineering/shipyard-QA franchise *and* a
contingent that deploys to MSC availabilities worldwide; the open question narrows
to whether that contingent has yet extended to the *new* Korea/Far-East
availabilities MSC's Indo-Pacific vision is creating. The operator-team's role is
**not direct-execute** (their lane is USS/SRF-JRMC, which does not overlap) but an
**internal, warm relationship-lead** — surfacing the Hanwha-driven growth in
Pacific USNS availabilities to their own task order's USNS contingent, far warmer
than a cold corporate introduction.

**Top risks (Assessment):** (1) the value pool is the *yards'* (steel/labor),
not the services layer, so the addressable CACI growth may be modest; (2) CACI's
existing MSC task order may already cover this with no net-new opportunity; (3)
the construction-side intersection runs into CFIUS/foreign-ownership sensitivity;
(4) Navy organic capacity (SURFMEPP/RMC) may absorb the planning work.

**Recommendation:** still TBD — this is Gate-1 identify-stage research. Operator
owns the call on whether to advance.

### 10.1 Thread A — Cyber / CMMC / supply-chain assurance, Hanwha-facing

- **CACI fit (Assessment):** Cyber area — network defense, Continuous Diagnostics and Mitigation (CDM), supply-chain risk management (SCRM). CACI has strong federal cyber past performance (CDM DEFEND, FEDND DEFEND A appear in the top-48 awards).

### 10.3 What Hanwha appears to be looking for (demand-side) — and the CACI filter

Lead: researched 2026-06-07 from Hanwha's hiring, the $5B-investment detail, and
CEO David Kim's statements. The honest headline — **most of what Hanwha needs is
not a CACI opportunity.** The narrow band where Hanwha's demand and CACI's
capabilities actually meet is US-Navy-market navigation for a foreign-owned
builder, which is exactly where §10.1 sits — and also where the OCI / foreign-
ownership constraint is sharpest.

**What Hanwha is looking for (FACT, from its own signals):**

- **Workforce at scale** — toward ~10,000 over five years (from ~1,800); a 39-month apprenticeship scaling from ~120 to ~500 graduates/year; the biggest stated hurdle is a **shortage of U.S. instructors** (it sent ~50 Korean trainers to Philadelphia) [s.2026-06-07-inquirer-david-kim-needs] [s.2026-06-07-how-hanwha-philly-shipyard-pla].
- **"Smart yard" automation transferred from Korea** — robotic welding, automated inspection, modular construction, digital-twin modeling — imported from Hanwha Ocean's Korean model [s.2026-06-07-how-hanwha-philly-shipyard-pla] [s.2026-06-07-usni-5b-philly].
- **Physical infrastructure** — two docks + three quays; Dry Dock 5 restoration (~$300–400M); cranes, sea gate/pumps, shops [s.2026-06-07-inquirer-david-kim-needs] [s.2026-06-07-usni-5b-philly].
- **A U.S. naval supply chain** — building/qualifying a domestic supply base and bringing Korean component makers into it [s.2026-06-07-how-hanwha-philly-shipyard-pla].
- **U.S. Navy market access** — "in discussions with the Navy" on combat vessels, targeting frigates and even Virginia-class submarine module work; ~$1.7B in pending orders [s.2026-06-07-inquirer-david-kim-needs] [s.2026-06-07-usni-5b-philly].

**The CACI-relevance filter (Assessment):**

- **Not CACI's lane:** trade workforce (welders/outfitters), drydock/crane infrastructure, and — importantly — the smart-yard automation, which is a Hanwha *strength it imports*, not a gap. That **materially downgrades the "sell Philly digital transformation to Hanwha" idea**: the yard's digital edge is self-supplied. (AI/VR training is a thin CACI adjacency at most.)
- **The overlap band:** the Navy-market-navigation layer a foreign-owned builder must clear to reach combat-vessel/submarine work — security and **CMMC** (Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification) compliance, supply-chain risk management for the naval supply base, **FOCI** (Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence) mitigation context, ITAR/export-control, and program/capture support. This validates §10.1's *direction* and is the only place Hanwha's demand and CACI's capabilities genuinely meet.
- **Posture caveat (important):** Hanwha is importing its own playbook (Korean tech + trainers) and partnering narrowly (VARD on NGLS design; U.S. industrial suppliers like the Cleveland-Cliffs steel mill). It is **not** visibly shopping for U.S. services/compliance partners. A CACI move here would be **proactive** — offering a need they have not put out to bid — not a response to a stated requirement. Harder, longer sell.

**Implication:** the demand research tightens §10. The Hanwha-*facing* opportunity narrows to compliance / security / SCRM (§10.1), is unsolicited, and is gated by OCI/FOCI; the broader "digital/services to the yard" idea is weak because the yard's digital edge is self-supplied. The stronger Hanwha-*driven* opportunity remains the government-side one (§10.2), where the **Navy — not Hanwha — is the buyer.**
