## Summary

CACI's own announcement of a $174M MSC task order to optimize MSC's machinery and
structural maintenance program — the contract that puts CACI engineers into
shipyards during MSC ship availabilities. This is the concrete bridge between
CACI's existing book of business and the Korea-based MRO trend: as MSC moves

## Extracted content

Date: September 29, 2022. Value: $174 million. Customer: U.S. Navy Military Sealift Command (MSC).

Scope — research, analysis, and development to optimize MSC's machinery and structural maintenance program:
- Reliability, Availability, Maintainability, and Safety (RAMS) engineering
- Life-cycle engineering Research, Development, Test, and Evaluation (RDT&E)
- Quality assurance planning and analysis
- Condition-Based Maintenance (CBM) research and analysis
- Goal: "reduce ownership costs and technical risks while improving fleet safety, reliability, availability, and readiness."

Work locations: primarily Washington, D.C. and Norfolk, Virginia, with **"worldwide deployments to shipyards during MSC ship availabilities."**

Contract vehicle: Department of Defense Information Analysis Center (DoD IAC) multiple-award contract (MAC), awarded by the U.S. Air Force's 774th Enterprise Sourcing Squadron. (This explains why the USAspending record for the task order — PIID FA807522F0054, ~$125.6M obligated — shows an Air Force awarding office with Navy funding.)

> "Joint warfighters rely on MSC to deliver agile logistics, strategic sealift, and specialized missions anywhere in the world and at any time." — John Mengucci, CACI President and CEO.
