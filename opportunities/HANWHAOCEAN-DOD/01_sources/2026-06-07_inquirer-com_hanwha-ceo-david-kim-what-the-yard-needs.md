## Summary

Philadelphia Inquirer interview with Hanwha Philly Shipyard CEO David Kim — the
clearest open-source statement of what the yard actually needs as it scales.
Demand-side intel for the §10 opportunity threads: the needs are dominated by
workforce, automation Hanwha imports from Korea, and physical infrastructure —
with US Navy market access as the strategic prize. Retrieved via WebFetch.

## Extracted content

> Content retrieved via the WebFetch tool on 2026-06-07. Verify any verbatim quote against the live page before brief use.

**Workforce & training (the dominant need):** ~1,800 workers/contractors today;
aims to add ~3,000 over the next decade (other reporting cites a 10,000 target on
a longer horizon). Seeking "reliable U.S. employees to train" for jobs up to ~$40/hr
plus overtime; apprenticeships for work with "sophisticated heating, hauling, and
cutting tools and robots." Shortage of U.S. instructors is the biggest hurdle; sent
~50 Korean trainers to Philadelphia; AI and virtual-reality training planned for
vehicles and painting.

**Technology / automation (largely Hanwha-supplied):** welding automation (up to
+30% productivity), robots to move steel plates (steel from Cleveland-Cliffs,
Coatesville), drones and mobile-device data input — "smart yard" methods
transferred from Hanwha's Korean yards.

**Infrastructure (cap-ex):** ~$300–400M to restore Dry Dock 5; cranes, sea gate
and pumps, shops; updated the Grand Block; possible new manufacturing facility at
submarine or surface level.

**Ships planned:** LNG-powered vessels (via Korean tech transfer), Jones Act
tankers, auxiliary/supply/support vessels, unmanned watercraft, and — the prize —
combat vessels for the Navy (in discussions); exploring submarine module work.
~$1.7B in pending orders.

**Challenges Kim names:** "Navy shipbuilding is in crisis … falling behind the
Chinese Navy, falling behind schedule, and over budget"; finding trained U.S.
workers; building LNG-carrier capacity that "does not exist in the U.S."; uncertainty
around government subsidies/incentives/grants; Trump-administration shipbuilding-office
staffing turnover.

**About Kim:** former McKinsey partner; prior HP and LG; joined Hanwha 2017; born/raised
in Orange, Texas.
