# Finalizing the Command Governance Framework for the askSage AI Platform: A Comprehensive Advisory and Strategic Analysis for SRF-JRMC
The rapid, localized integration of artificial intelligence within ship repair and maintenance activities presents a profound governance challenge for the U.S. Naval Ship Repair Facility and Japan Regional Maintenance Center (SRF-JRMC). While generative AI capabilities promise to reclaim significant workforce hours and streamline administrative and technical workflows, they must be deployed within a highly regulated cybersecurity and records management framework.
As the command reviews the draft of SRF-JRMC Instruction 5239.1, establishing a defensible, compliant, and operationally resilient posture requires resolving a series of open regulatory placeholders, validating platform accreditations, and aligning with Navy-wide enterprise directives. This report provides the necessary policy determinations, technical resolutions, and strategic recommendations to transition the draft instruction into an executable, legally compliant command directive.
## Executive Assessment of the Localized AI Governance Landscape
Ship repair operations at SRF-JRMC require extreme operational agility, yet they must be executed under strict adherence to technical specifications, safety protocols, and information security standards. The command's dual-platform strategy—employing both GenAI.mil and the askSage platform—leverages the distinct strengths of each tool.
While GenAI.mil serves as the enterprise default environment for general inquiry and basic research, askSage is retained to execute specialized, agentic workflows. These agentic workflows involve multi-step reasoning, direct file ingestion from naval network shares, and the synthesis of complex engineering and logistics drafts.
However, maintaining a decentralized, platform-specific instruction for askSage requires a robust legal and technical defense. This defense is necessary because the Department of the Navy (DON) has enacted strict consolidation mandates designed to eliminate redundant, non-enterprise software environments.
By defining clear human-in-the-loop technical boundaries, establishing strict data controls, and aligning localized procedures with national and departmental policies, this instruction provides the necessary framework to defend the command's use of askSage.
## Reference Validation and Federal Compliance Mappings
To ensure that SRF-JRMC Instruction 5239.1 is defensible on its face, the working group must verify and formally integrate several federal, departmental, and command-level references. These references establish the legal and regulatory boundaries for cybersecurity, records retention, and information security.
### Verified Reference Identifiers
The working group has verified the draft's placeholder references against active military and federal directives.
 * **DoD Instruction 5400.19**: Promulgated on July 28, 2025, by the Office of the Assistant to the Secretary of Defense for Public Affairs, this instruction establishes the policy governance framework and limitations for utilizing artificial intelligence within DoD public affairs activities. It outlines five guiding principles for AI utilization: mission relevance, understanding benefits and limitations, upholding legal and ethical standards, maintaining security and responsible AI use, and establishing strict transparency and oversight. The directive requires that any visual or textual information created or edited with generative AI for public release must carry an explicit disclosure citing the technology's use, and that components must implement human oversight plans to review and approve all outputs prior to release.
 * **CDAO Generative AI Guidelines**: The Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Officer (CDAO) issued the memorandum *Guidelines and Guardrails to Inform Governance of Generative Artificial Intelligence* on July 12, 2024. This policy has since been operationalized through the public launch of the CDAO "GenAI Toolkit". The toolkit progresses sequentially through the product development lifecycle, providing project leaders with 33 specialized risk assessment tools designed to manage generative AI deployments. CDAO co-chairs the White House Chief AI Officer Council Working Group on Risk Management to coordinate compliance with Office of Management and Budget (OMB) Memorandum M-24-10 and National Security Memorandum 25 (NSM-25).
 * **DON CIO GenAI.mil Guidance**: The Assistant Secretary of the Navy for Research, Development, and Acquisition (ASN(RD&A)) and the DON Chief Information Officer formally issued a joint memorandum on January 28, 2026, designating GenAI.mil as the Naval Enterprise IT Service for Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) and Impact Level 5 (IL5) workloads. A subsequent DON CIO memorandum, issued on March 16, 2026, details the Department's strategy for accelerating AI adoption, utilizing free training resources (such as Google Skills, Microsoft Copilot, LinkedIn Learning, and the Naval Postgraduate School's Harnessing AI Course), and measuring exact time savings.
 * **36 CFR Part 1229**: Under the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) regulations, Part 1229 governs the emergency disposal of federal records. Specifically, **36 CFR § 1229.10** dictates the mandatory notification and joint determination procedures required when records present a continuing menace to human health, life, or property. Additionally, **36 CFR § 1229.12** provides the legal authority to destroy records outside the territorial limits of the United States during a state of war or imminent hostile action, requiring a formal report to NARA within six months of the destruction event. These emergency provisions are critical to the command's security spillage cleanup workflow.

## The Enterprise Compliance Paradox and Cattle Drive Risk
A major administrative challenge for the instruction is the potential conflict with the joint ASN(RD&A) and DON CIO memorandum issued on January 28, 2026. This memorandum formally designated GenAI.mil as the mandated enterprise generative AI platform for Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) and Impact Level 5 (IL5) workloads.
Crucially, the directive ordered all Navy commands and activities to transition their generative AI operations to GenAI.mil **no later than April 30, 2026**. Furthermore, it initiated the consolidation of redundant software environments under the Navy's **Cattle Drive Initiative**.
Because the April 30, 2026 transition deadline has passed, the command’s ongoing operation of askSage represents a potential compliance exception. To defend this posture against Cattle Drive audits, the command must document a clear "agentic" capability justification.
While GenAI.mil currently provides a robust conversational chat interface, secure file uploads (PDF, DOCX, TXT, PPTX), and basic Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG), it lacks the advanced agentic autonomy required for complex, multi-step shipyard planning.
The askSage platform excels in these specific workflows, offering label-based access control, multi-model flexibility (utilizing Azure Gov OpenAI, Mistral, Google Gemini, and AWS Bedrock), and the ability to operate across geographically redundant environments.
To maintain compliance, the CIO must register the askSage use case with the DON CIO GenAI Task Force, citing this technical gap as the justification for a temporary waiver. The command's long-term plan must be to transfer these agentic workflows to GenAI.mil once the enterprise platform matures to support equivalent autonomous capabilities.
## Technical Tensions in Controlled Data Enclaves
Evaluating the data boundaries in the draft instruction reveals two technical developments that require updating the policy's restrictions.
### Reconciling the PHI/PII Restriction
The draft instruction establishes a strict carve-out prohibiting Personally Identifiable Information (PII) and Protected Health Information (PHI) from being processed by askSage, aligning with GenAI.mil enterprise constraints.
However, on December 9, 2025, Ask Sage signed an enterprise-wide agreement with the Defense Health Agency (DHA) to deploy generative AI capabilities across clinical, administrative, and operational workflows. Under this agreement, Ask Sage became the first and only generative AI platform to receive an Impact Level 5 (IL5) authorization to process Protected Health Information (PHI).
Additionally, its approved federal Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA) outlines the secure collection of limited user PII (names, email addresses, and login timestamps) to support authentication, usage monitoring, and auditing.
The working group must update Section 4.b to clarify that the prohibition of PHI and PII is a localized command risk-tolerance decision—driven by a desire to prevent compilation and aggregation risks on industrial ship repair networks—rather than a platform-level security limitation of askSage itself.
### Upgrading the Naval Nuclear Propulsion Information Boundary
Historically, Naval Nuclear Propulsion Information (NNPI) has been strictly isolated on dedicated, physically separated servers. Consequently, Section 4.b of the draft instruction implements a blanket prohibition on processing NNPI.
However, in late 2024, PEO Digital and NAVSEA 08 successfully deployed Microsoft Purview Information Protection (MPIP) sensitivity labels and object-level security controls within the Flank Speed tenant. This security update allows authorized users within the NNPI Community of Interest (COI) to migrate, store, and collaboratively process Unclassified NNPI (U-NNPI) directly within the secure Flank Speed cloud.
By maintaining an absolute ban on NNPI, the draft instruction prevents the command from leveraging agentic AI to automate and optimize nuclear ship and submarine maintenance.
The working group should modify Section 4.b to permit the ingestion of U-NNPI into askSage, provided that:
 1. The specific askSage instance is fully integrated with Flank Speed's MPIP sensitivity labeling system.
 2. The workflow complies with the data migration and user requirements managed by the local NNPI Champions and the U-NNPI Knowledge Center.
## Electronic Spillage Incident Handling and Emergency Records Destruction
A major compliance issue in the draft instruction is the conflict between security spillage remediation and federal records retention laws.
Under standard operating procedures, all automated prompts, agentic chain-of-thought sequences, and interaction logs represent federal records. These logs serve as decision-support artifacts that must be permanently retained for compliance and potential investigations (such as JAGMAN or safety mishap reviews) in accordance with SECNAV M-5210.1 and SECNAVINST 5239.19A.
However, if a user accidentally enters classified national security information or NNPI into an unclassified askSage session, this action constitutes an electronic spillage. The spilled material immediately contaminates the IL5 environment, creating a severe cybersecurity violation.
Under OPNAVSTAFFINST 5510.168, the ISSM must immediately isolate the session logs and suspend the user's account pending a Preliminary Inquiry (PI) by the Facility Security Officer (FSO), with the violation formally recorded in the Defense Counterintelligence and Security System (DISS).
This creates a conflict: records management laws require preserving the logs, while cybersecurity and information security program directives (SECNAVINST 5510.36B) mandate the immediate destruction of the spilled classified data to eliminate the security threat.
To resolve this conflict, the instruction must leverage the legal authority of **36 CFR § 1229.10** (emergency destruction of records posing a continuing menace) and the emergency destruction provisions of **SECNAV M-5210.1 (Part I, Section 5)**.
By declaring the contaminated unclassified database index a "continuing menace to property or national security," the Commanding Officer is authorized to bypass normal retention timelines and execute immediate destruction of the spilled data.

### Outcome Measurement
Consistent with the March 16, 2026 DON CIO mandate, the command must actively measure and report time savings. Department Heads must establish baseline times for routine tasks when performed without AI assistance.
Once the askSage agentic workflows are implemented, personnel must record their assisted task completion times and log these metrics through the official DON AI Time Savings Survey portal (https://forms.osi.apps.mil/r/FQjbJxu2y0).
The resulting data supports the Navy’s broader "Cattle Drive" initiative and helps qualify high-performing teams for recognition under the DON AI Efficiency Challenge.
