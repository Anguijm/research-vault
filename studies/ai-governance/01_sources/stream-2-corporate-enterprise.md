## GROUP 3 — Foundational cross-industry frameworks (fetched)

**F1. NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)** — NIST. AI RMF 1.0 Jan 26, 2023; Generative AI Profile (NIST-AI-600-1) July 26, 2024. Public.
URLs: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework and https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/
Four functions:
- **GOVERN** (cross-cutting): transparent policies/processes; roles, responsibilities, communication lines; executive-leadership accountability; risk tolerance; system inventory; safe decommissioning; workforce training; risk culture with incident sharing; external-feedback integration; supply-chain/third-party risk.
- **MAP:** document intended purpose, deployment context, categorize systems, weigh costs/benefits and impact likelihood/magnitude.
- **MEASURE:** quantitative + qualitative metrics, evaluate trustworthiness, track risks over time, validate in context.
- **MANAGE:** prioritize risks; mitigate/transfer/avoid/accept; monitor third parties; document incident response; continuous improvement.
Verbatim (AIRC Core page): "Govern is a cross-cutting function that is infused throughout AI risk management and enables the other functions of the process." / "Senior leadership sets the tone for risk management within an organization, and with it, organizational culture." / "Documentation can enhance transparency, improve human review processes, and bolster accountability in AI system teams."

**F2. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — AI Management System (AIMS)** — ISO/IEC. Summary fetched from Schellman, "ISO/IEC 42001 Certification Requirements Explained" (Oct 21, 2024; updated Jun 16, 2026): https://www.schellman.com/blog/iso-certifications/what-are-iso-42001-requirements ; standard itself https://www.iso.org/standard/42001 (paid).
Requires an AIMS over clauses 4–10 (context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement): top-management commitment + documented AI policy integrated with strategy; AI risk assessment + AI impact assessment before deployment; Annex A controls across the lifecycle; third-party management; continual improvement; transparency/fairness/accountability; certification via independent third-party audit.
Verbatim (Schellman): "Top management should contribute to the establishment of your AI policy, its communication to your wider organization, and its integration."

**F3. EU AI Act — risk-tiering (context)** — source fetched: Future of Life Institute high-level summary (updated 30 May 2024): https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/high-level-summary/
Four tiers: Unacceptable (prohibited; enforceable since 2 Feb 2025); High-risk (most obligations: lifecycle risk-management system, data governance, technical documentation, automatic logging, human oversight, accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity, quality-management system, conformity assessment + EU registration); Limited (transparency only, Art. 50); Minimal (no requirements). GPAI providers carry documentation/copyright/training-summary duties; systemic-risk models add adversarial testing + incident tracking.
Verbatim (FLI): "The AI Act classifies AI according to its risk: Unacceptable risk is prohibited…Most of the text addresses high-risk AI systems, which are regulated."
**Unverified lead (search snippet only):** Digital Omnibus (provisional agreement 7 May 2026) reportedly defers the high-risk Annex-III deadline from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027; penalties up to €35M or 7% of global turnover. Verify before relying.

## Top takeaways (corporate/enterprise)

1. A standing cross-functional governance body (board-approved policy + monthly committee) is the universal anchor.
2. Named single-point accountability, increasingly an executive or CEO (CEO oversight tied to the largest EBIT impact in McKinsey's survey; "single accountable executive per high-risk system").
3. A model/use-case inventory bound to risk tiering is foundational plumbing ("inventory it and identity-bind it").
4. Risk tiering drives proportional controls — light touch for low-risk, hard gates for high-risk (EU AI Act formalizes it; enterprises mirror it internally).
5. Governance is a lifecycle discipline with gates, not a one-time sign-off (impact assessments before development).
6. The dominant operating model is "centralized governance, federated execution"; many start centralized, then federate as maturity grows.
7. AI governance is built on existing model-risk and enterprise-risk machinery; NIST AI RMF + ISO 42001 + EU AI Act are the de facto reference stack.
8. Third-party / vendor model risk is a named, recurring control area.
9. Agentic AI is the live frontier reshaping all of the above.
