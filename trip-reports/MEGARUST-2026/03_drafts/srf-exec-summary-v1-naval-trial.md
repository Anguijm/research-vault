2.  SRF-JRMC named directly. SRF-Japan is on the active list for the NSWC Carderock Composite Work Cell program.

    a.  Dr. Maureen Foley (NSWC Carderock) showed a slide on Day 1 listing the six maintenance centers her team is currently working with: MARMC, SWRMC, NWRMC, Rota, SRF-Japan, and Pearl Harbor. The program ships heated wire bending tables, composite sheet stock, and waterjet cutting equipment to the maintenance centers so they can build composite ship parts locally — anti-siphon guards, gauge panels, deck-edge scuppers, RSL sunshields. 3.  SWRMC's Pacific expeditionary maintenance container program — SRF Japan as Area Maintenance Commander.

    a.  Robinson's Day-3 keynote described an unfunded mission, driven by Pat McDermott, involving approximately 50 expeditionary maintenance containers ("shops in the box") pre-staged across the Pacific for battle damage repair on surface ships. Tenders for the surface force do not exist and are not in the near-term shipbuilding plan; this container program is the substitute capability. Containers are activated by SWRMC sailors and civilians flown in from San Diego, can stay where pre-positioned or move to an austere location, and currently support single-ship two-week deployments.

    b.  Robinson said SWRMC is "actually sending project teams for contract oversight, working with SRF Japan, which is, of course, the Area Maintenance Commander out there, to support these things." So SWRMC project teams come out to coordinate with SRF Japan in its Area Maintenance Commander role on the container deployments.

    c.  The exact division of work between SWRMC and SRF-JRMC on this container mission — and how it ties to the related Battle Damage Exercises (BDX) — is not visible from Robinson's keynote. This is an internal SRF question worth a conversation with the wartime-readiness group and the chief engineer.

    d.  Robinson named operational realities that complicate the mission: US Freon cannot be shipped overseas (so AC units have been modified to swap Freon before deployment); calibration gases used for gas-free devices cannot move by airfreight; and a broader category of items in these containers do not ship by standard air. Whatever the right SRF posture turns out to be, getting visibility into what SWRMC has worked through is worth pursuing.

4.  Active NAVSEA coating spec updates affecting SRF-JRMC work.

    a.  Per reference (b), Mark Ingle (NAVSEA 05P2) walked through the top five updates in Standard Item 009-032 FY-28 Change 1, published 31 Mar 2026. NAVSEA adopted 49 of 79 industry-proposed changes:

        (1) A new zinc-rich paint option (MIL-DTL-24441 Type III Formula 159) added to Table 2 Lines 1B/1C, giving cathodic protection where the coating is scratched.

        (2) A new biocide-boosted foul-release antifouling (MIL-PRF-24647E Type IV), alternative to copper, up to 7-year service life.

        (3) A streamlined waterfront process for two-coat interior topcoats (paragraph 3.1.16).

        (4) A new coating note for magazines and ammunition holds (MIL-DTL-24441 Type IV Formula 152 white epoxy, for magazines holding saluting charges).

        (5) Fluidized-bed powder coating expanded to airtight doors, watertight doors, hatches, and scuttles.

    b.  These are direct changes to the surface-prep and coating work SRF-JRMC teams do. Worth a focused review by SRF technical authority to identify where the changes affect current SRF-Japan work.

    c.  Per reference (c), MIL-PRF-16173F adds Class 8 "Persistent" preservatives. Class 8 is for spaces where it is not practical to install a coating — rudder interiors, bilge keels, voids. Publication of 16173F sat for nine months (Aug 2025 to late May 2026) while NAVAIR and NAVSEA fought over the spec's format; that dispute was resolved approximately one week before MegaRust. Class 8 codifies maintenance approaches sailors have already been using in the field.

5.  Anti-Rust Program.

    a.  Mark Lattner (NAVSEA 05P) reported the program kicked off Jan 2026. Numbers since then: 85 ship maintenance availabilities worked, approximately 60,000 square feet of preservation completed, more than 3,000 additively-manufactured parts delivered to ships. The program runs on two tracks:

        (1) CCAT (Corrosion Control Assistance Teams) — the established sailor-training-and-tools model, 16 years old, nine locations worldwide. SRF has a CCAT team out, placing SRF-JRMC in the program footprint on the CCAT side.

        (2) SWARM Teams (Swift Warship Action on Rust Mitigation) — contractor crews doing production preservation work during short availabilities, working areas sailors cannot easily reach.

    b.  Whether SWARM Team coverage extends to SRF-Japan is a separate question. I can raise it with the program POCs if requested.

    c.  On Day 1, Andrew Sheets (Marine Corps) and Ian Shannon (Navy aviation) made a structural observation: once contractors take on preservation work, it becomes hard to give that work back to sailors later because sailors stop learning the trade. On Day 2, Lattner addressed this directly — the Anti-Rust Program structures SWARM Teams to work alongside sailors rather than take work over from them.

6.  SIMA San Diego stood up on Day 3 (4 Jun 2026) at a 1430 ceremony at Naval Station 32nd Street, Building 3116.

    a.  SIMA (Shore Intermediate Maintenance Activity) is a structural reorganization driven by a December 2025 CNO mandate. NAVSEA (Vice Admiral James P. Downey) tasked CNRMC (Rear Admiral Daniel L. Lannaman) to direct SWRMC and MARMC to split off their Production Departments (Code 900) into separate SIMAs.

    b.  Approximately 900 sailors moved from SWRMC to SIMA San Diego that day. CO is Captain Bill Albert. Full separation of the parent organizations runs through Jun 2029.

7.  Combat-systems corrosion as a warfighting problem (Duldulao keynote, Day 2).

    a.  CAPT Richard Duldulao (NSWC Port Hueneme Chief Engineer, MIL) framed combat-systems corrosion in warfighting terms: "When a part of the combat system degrades, the kill chain potentially weakens."

    b.  Cost data from his slides:

        (1) $14M to replace one SPY-1 array. Three arrays per DDG.

        (2) $450K to replace one FCS MK 99 director plus the alignment and collimation work that follows.

        (3) $500K to resurface one AEM/S mask.

    c.  Named hulls with FCS director corrosion replacements during recent availabilities: USS Wayne E. Meyer (DDG-108) and USS Cole (DDG-67), both East Coast.

    d.  FY25 INSURV data: approximately 900 combat-systems deficiencies attributed directly to corrosion, more than 660 of which degraded mission capability.

    e.  Some RAM launchers in the active fleet have train bearings seized so badly the launcher cannot slew.

8.  NSWC PHD uses a different coating spec than NAVSEA 05P2.

    a.  In Q&A during Duldulao's keynote, Tim Tenopir (NSWC PHD, Office of Engineering and Technology) said NSWC PHD has moved off MIL-PRF-23236 and MIL-PRF-24635 polysiloxane for combat-systems composite structures (AEM/S, radar enclosures) and uses MIL-PRF-85285 (aviation-derived polyurethane) instead.

    b.  Mark Ingle's coatings work at NAVSEA 05P2 stays on the 23236/24635 polysiloxane track for HM&E (hull, mechanical, electrical) topside work. So two parallel coating tracks are in use.

    c.  Tenopir also mentioned that "some future LPDs don't have AEM/S anymore" — implying the AEM/S structure is being dropped from some upcoming LPD hulls. If SRF-JRMC sees LPDs with AEM/S, the two-track spec split matters to SRF-JRMC's work.

9.  Broader policy context.

    a.  Vice Admiral Dave Lewis (ASNE President) opened Day 2 with a keynote on PAE acquisition reform. For 70 years, the Navy organizations that buy ships have not been the same organizations that maintain them later. That separation is why corrosion-prevention work has been chronically underfunded. The new Portfolio Acquisition Executive (PAE) construct puts the buyer and the maintainer under the same person, with contracting, engineering, and management in one place. Lewis directed the audience: "Go find your PAE and make nice with that person — they now are the solution to our corrosion issues."

    b.  Per reference (a), §345 is the standing policy backdrop. Conlan Hsu (NSRP) cited §345 at the opening of the Day-1 NSRP SPC panel meeting. Several program activities at MegaRust align with §345: the Anti-Rust Program, NSWC Port Hueneme's NDI work, the Paint Center of Excellence (PCoE) shipping equipment to maintenance centers, and SIMA San Diego standing up.

    c.  The DOD annual cost-of-corrosion report stopped in 2020. Dr. Walter A. Juzukonis (AFLCMC) reported that the LMI / Eric Hertzberg data pipeline that produced the figure is no longer available. Paul Chang (DOD Corrosion Policy Oversight Office) said the restart is "in the works." Any current Navy or DOD corrosion-cost figure being quoted is using pre-2020 data.

    d.  Surface Team One (ST-1) has a Pacific presence including Japan. James Holman runs ST-1's Corrosion Community of Practice and separately works for SURFMEPP. ST-1 has a new contract effective 1 May 2026, 80 new hires brought on Nov 2025, and people stationed in Hawaii, Japan, and San Diego. The Japan-based ST-1 POC name is pending Holman's shared-drive lookup.
