# MegaRust 2026 — CACI Executive Summary (v0.3)

**Status:** Full three-day conference now covered. I attended ASNE MegaRust 2026 in San Diego across 2026-06-02, 2026-06-03, and the morning of 2026-06-04. Citations point to source-ledger entries.

## Why this trip matters for CACI BD

MegaRust 2026 is the annual ASNE Navy-and-industry conference on ship corrosion and preservation — a 20-year-old forum that this year added a cross-service DOD Corrosion Programs Panel, replacing the 2025 DOD Corrosion Conference that didn't happen. The panel pulled corrosion-program leads from Navy aviation, Marine Corps, Air Force, and Army into one room.

The trip surfaced real shifts in how NAVSEA and the broader DOD are reorganizing sustainment work — shifts that change where the money flows and who has authority over what. I attended in person and captured ten substantive session-audio recordings (Day 1: morning NSRP SPC panel, afternoon DOD Corrosion panel, afternoon AM/composites session; Day 2: morning NAVSEA 05P/05P2 brief, Duldulao keynote, afternoon Robotics/Drones technical track; Day 3: SWRMC Robinson keynote on standing up SIMA San Diego).

## The policy lens — NDAA §345

The 2026 NDAA, §345, signed 18 December 2025 as Public Law 119-60, tells the Secretary of the Navy to investigate and adopt advanced surface-ship-maintenance technology. Drone-based inspection for uncrewed structural surveys is the specifically prioritized category. Adoption is mandatory; the section itself names no dollar amounts, deadlines, or specific programs.

Several program activities at MegaRust line up with §345 — the Anti-Rust Program, NSWC Port Hueneme's NDI work, the Paint Center of Excellence (PCoE) shipping equipment to maintenance centers, and SIMA San Diego standing up to consolidate sailor-self-sufficiency capability. None of the speakers cited §345 by name on tape. (Source for the statute: my pre-trip prep; verified via Holland & Knight FY26 NDAA analysis and S.1071 119th Congress.)

## Four structural BD signals

**1. PAE acquisition reform is the new center of gravity for sustainment money.** Vice Admiral Dave Lewis (ASNE President) opened Day 2 with a direct fix for a 70-year problem: the Navy organizations that buy ships have never been the same organizations that have to maintain them later. That separation is why corrosion-prevention investment has been chronically underfunded. The new Portfolio Acquisition Executive (PAE) construct puts buyer and maintainer under one person, with contracting, engineering, and management in one place. Lewis told the audience: "Go find your PAE and make nice with that person — they now are the solution to our corrosion issues."

**What this means for CACI:** for any CACI sustainment pursuit, the PAE for that portfolio is the new relationship target. Worth an internal mapping pass. (Source: clip 7.)

**2. SIMA San Diego stood up Day 3 — Code 900 split off from SWRMC, ~900 sailors moved.** Robinson's Day-3 keynote announced the 1430 stand-up ceremony at Naval Station 32nd Street, Building 3116. Driven by a December 2025 CNO mandate, executed NAVSEA → CNRMC → SWRMC/MARMC. Captain Bill Albert is CO. Same restructuring is happening at MARMC on the Atlantic side. Full unraveling runs through June 2029.

**What this means for CACI:** SIMA San Diego is a new structural touchpoint over the 2026-2029 transition window. New contracting footprint will emerge as the organization stands up. Worth tracking once the SIMA contracting office is named. (Source: clip 10.)

**3. The "we paid for proof of concept and then stopped funding it" problem showed up five times across the trip.** Day 1, clip 5: unidentified audience challenger telling the cross-service panel that depot-side corrosion findings don't reach Program Acquisition Executives. Day 1, clip 6: Dr. Maureen Foley — "Most of our stuff is all back fit, mainly because we can't get into the new acquisition." Day 2, clip 7: Vice Admiral Lewis's PAE keynote — the policy-level acknowledgment and named fix. Day 2, clip 8: Tim Tenopir's "ten rolls of duct tape" laser-weapon prototype story. Day 3, clip 10: John Robinson framing expeditionary maintenance as an unfunded mission SWRMC is doing anyway because the fleet needs it.

Five independent surfacings across Vice-Admiral to ED to ISEA to senior-engineer to staff-scientist levels. Pattern is recognized broadly inside the Navy. (Sources: clips 5, 6, 7, 8, 10.)

**4. The cost-of-corrosion data pipeline is dead.** Dr. Walter A. Juzukonis (AFLCMC) told the Day-1 cross-service panel that the DOD's annual cost-of-corrosion report stopped in 2020. The LMI / Eric Hertzberg data pipeline that produced the figure is gone. Paul Chang (DOD Corrosion Policy Oversight Office) said the restart is "in the works." Any current CACI brief quoting a Navy or DOD corrosion cost figure is using pre-2020 data. (Source: clip 5.)

## Active programs and procurement pathways

**The Anti-Rust Program is post-launch.** Mark Lattner (NAVSEA 05P, the SES running non-rotating-equipment work) reported the program kicked off January 2026. Since then: 85 ship availabilities worked, about 60,000 square feet of preservation done, more than 3,000 additively-manufactured parts delivered to ships. Two tracks: CCAT (Corrosion Control Assistance Teams) and SWARM Teams (contractor crews). Industry Day is 14-15 July in Norfolk VA; POC is Josh Chapman at NCMS. Day-2 robotics technical track surfaced four contractor capability categories actively being evaluated. (Sources: clips 7, 9.)

**Mark Ingle (NAVSEA 05P2) is the senior coatings technical authority at NAVSEA 05.** His organization sits inside Lattner's 05P. The Paint Center of Excellence (PCoE) at NSWC Carderock is the program house — Kylee Fazende is Acting Program Manager. PCoE is driving MIL-PRF-16173F (Class 8 Persistent Preservatives), MIL-PRF-24712B Amendment 1, Standard Item 009-032 FY-28 Change 1, heated-wire-bending-table deployment to all RMCs. (Sources: clips 6, 7.)

**SWRMC's advanced manufacturing program has a named gap.** Robinson rejected "additive manufacturing" framing in favor of "advanced manufacturing" = CNC + AM + reverse engineering. He called reverse engineering the Achilles heel — engineers with proper tools to take a broken part with no drawings and produce CAD-ready drawings. A DMG MORI laser-deposit machine is months into commissioning at SWRMC. Vice Admiral McLane (Naval Surface Forces) pushes Captain Kerosich (SWRMC) weekly on AM progress. (Source: clip 10.)

**Surface Team One (ST-1) has Pacific presence, a fresh contract, and reach into multiple organizations.** James Holman runs ST-1; SURFMEPP is his separate employer. New contract effective 1 May 2026, 80 new hires brought on November 2025, people stationed in Hawaii, Japan, and San Diego. First ships are DDG 2.0 mods (East Coast + West Coast). Dr. Armen Kvryan at NSWC Port Hueneme is also on ST-1. (Sources: clips 6, 8, 9.)

**SWRMC operates a Pacific-theater expeditionary maintenance container program with SRF-JRMC.** Robinson described approximately 50 containers ("shops in the box") pre-staged across the Pacific for battle damage repair, currently unfunded, Pat McDermott driving. Robinson explicitly described this as co-responsibility with SRF-JRMC. Operationally adjacent to BDR-FLEET-READINESS work. (Source: clip 10.)

**Combat-systems corrosion is a separate domain from HM&E topside corrosion, with different specs and different program offices.** NSWC Carderock + PCoE work the HM&E (hull, mechanical, electrical) topside track — Standard Item 009-032, MIL-PRF-23236 epoxy, MIL-PRF-24635 polysiloxane. NSWC Port Hueneme works the combat-systems track — AEGIS, NSSMS, RAM, AEM/S, UNREP — under In-Service Engineering Agent (ISEA) and On Ship Refurbishment Program (OSRP) funding, with a different coating spec (MIL-PRF-85285, aviation polyurethane). CAPT Duldulao's Day-2 keynote put a warfighting frame on the combat-systems side: $14M per SPY-1 array replacement (three per DDG), $450K per FCS MK 99 director, $500K per AEM/S resurfacing, ~900 corrosion-attributed combat-systems deficiencies in FY25 INSURV data with more than 660 degrading mission capability. (Source: clip 8.)

## Industry seen at the Day-2 robotics track

Four capability categories the Anti-Rust Program is evaluating. None are CACI direct competitors; all are potential teaming or subcontracting candidates if a future CACI-led Navy maintenance contract needs scope in these areas.

- **SwRI (George Samet, Intelligent Systems Division)** — robotics state of the art. Samet's "data-to-action gap" framing: current inspection robots collect data but don't assess, plan, execute, or report. SwRI is in R&D phase, not production — not a CACI BD pursue.
- **Apellix (Jim Kunkle)** — spray-painting drones, semi-autonomous and autonomous. Three touchpoints at MegaRust (Day-1 NSRP pitch, Day-2 Lattner reference, Day-2 deep dive). Apellix is visibly working a NAVSEA engagement path.
- **Edify (Jeff Apt, global business and company manager)** — magnetic-crawler hull-thickness NDI with pulsed eddy current array and ultrasonic, deployable pierside or in a wet berth. Operational use case: deploy during a non-docking maintenance period, generate inspection report, use it to plan the next docking-availability scope — well ahead of the ship docking. Apt cited a CBO report on 14 years of dry-dock yard-period overruns as the data foundation: CBO publication 61940, "Maintenance Delays for Conventional Navy Ships," December 2025. Directly usable in any CACI brief arguing for pre-docking hull inspection capability.
- **PPG Protective and Marine Coatings (Jim McCarthy, Director Tech Ops US/Canada)** — electrostatic coating application. PPG supplies under MIL-PRF-24635F. McCarthy was pinch-hitting for Bart Dima.

(Source: clip 9.)

## NAVSEA 05 personnel context — Deferred Retirement Program churn

The civilian coatings authority side of NAVSEA 05 is short-staffed. Three of four named MegaRust contacts for the Anti-Rust Program are in "Acting" roles: Nate Livesey (Acting Program Manager), Bob Steele (Acting Deputy), Kylee Fazende (Acting PCoE Program Manager). Mark Ingle's organization chart has multiple unfilled positions, all driven by people taking the Deferred Retirement Program (DRP). Named recent departures: Jeff Duckworth (Philadelphia, retired); Liz Hasselbeck (retired, Cody Lieberman took over); Kevin Clutcher (DRPM Subs delegated authority — letter expired during an audit, was re-upped); Allison Jones (came back as a contractor after taking DRP, now working the 009-032 update).

**What this means for CACI:** NAVSEA 05's coatings organization is in active turnover, on top of the DRPM and PAE restructuring Lewis described, and the SIMA stand-ups at SWRMC and MARMC. Relationship-development openings are visible at multiple positions. (Sources: clips 7, 10.)

## What to watch / what to pursue

- **PAE mapping.** For each CACI sustainment pursuit (Navy surface ships, Navy aviation, Marine Corps), identify the relevant PAE. Lewis named it as the new relationship target.
- **SURFMEPP procurement history.** Fresh contract, Pacific presence, multiple organizational touchpoints. Worth a focused Opportunity Screening pull on SURFMEPP-routed solicitations.
- **SIMA San Diego contracting footprint.** New organization, new contracting touchpoints over 2026-2029 transition. Worth tracking once the SIMA contracting office is named.
- **Reverse engineering capability for SWRMC.** Robinson's named Achilles heel. If CACI has a reverse-engineering-tooling capability, the timing is favorable. (Note: operator's SRF-side play involves Amanda Keach, SRF-JRMC AM Program Manager — that's a separate channel from any CACI-direct engagement.)
- **NSWC Port Hueneme OOT direct-call invitation.** Tim Tenopir said on tape: "Armen and I will take calls any time of the day." For any CACI engagement on combat-systems coatings, the Office of Engineering and Technology (OOT) at NSWC Port Hueneme is the door.
- **Industry Day Norfolk 14-15 July.** I'm not attending personally. Remains a low-cost CACI BD-team-attendance candidate for the Anti-Rust Program demonstrations.
- **NDI vendor landscape for hidden corrosion.** Three independent organizations working the problem (Air Force drone-mapping, NSWC PHD thermography and fiber-optics, Edify pierside / wet-berth crawler). If a future CACI pursuit needs NDI capability, the vendor landscape is mappable.
