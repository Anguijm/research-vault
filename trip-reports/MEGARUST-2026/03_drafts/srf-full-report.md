## 3. SWRMC's Pacific expeditionary maintenance container program — SRF Japan as Area Maintenance Commander

This is the Day-3 narrative that most directly affects SRF-JRMC's operational mission.

### 3.1 What Robinson described in his keynote

John Robinson, Executive Director of Southwest Regional Maintenance Center (SWRMC), gave the Day-3 morning keynote. Inside the keynote, he described an unfunded mission that's being executed anyway because the fleet needs it.

The framing: tenders for the surface force don't exist and aren't in the near-term Navy shipbuilding plan. Surface ships in the Pacific need battle-damage-repair capability in theater, and there is no tender to provide it. So SWRMC has been pre-staging approximately **50 expeditionary maintenance containers** ("shops in the box") across the Pacific. Containers can stay where they're pre-positioned, or be moved to an austere location, and are activated by SWRMC sailors and civilians flown out from San Diego. Currently the program supports single-ship two-week deployments. The program lead is **Pat McDermott** — Robinson didn't expand on his organizational position.

Robinson on SRF-JRMC's role:

> "And it's more than just high level. We're actually sending project teams for contract oversight, working with SRF Japan, which of course is the Area Maintenance Commander out there to support these things."

### 3.2 Co-responsibility, not SWRMC-supports-SRF

Robinson named SRF Japan as the Area Maintenance Commander in the Western Pacific, with SWRMC project teams coming out to coordinate through that role on the container deployments. What's not visible from his keynote is the exact division of work between SWRMC and SRF-JRMC — whether SRF-JRMC is co-executing portions of the container mission, whether it's purely providing the maintenance-command authority that SWRMC works through, or some combination. The related Battle Damage Exercises (BDX) work also isn't visible from his keynote.

### 3.3 The operational realities are tricky

Robinson named one example: Freon used in air conditioning units in the US is not the same as Freon used overseas, and they're not allowed to ship US Freon overseas. So SWRMC has had to go back and modify AC units to swap Freon for the in-theater containers. His framing: "That's the stuff that would really suck to learn if you had to do this real time with kinetics flying around."

There's a broader pattern of items in these containers that don't ship by standard airfreight. Calibration gases used for gas-free devices — the kind of gases that have to be on hand at any maintenance site for safe entry into spaces — are "literally bad gas" and can't move by airfreight either. Other categories of container contents face similar shipping constraints. Planning for this mission means working those constraints through up front rather than discovering them in execution.

### 3.4 Why this is worth raising at SRF leadership level

If this is going to become more than two commands doing parallel work, SRF needs to engage with it deliberately. The internal pathway: SRF wartime-readiness group and the SRF chief engineer are the natural touchpoints. Strategic framing: working with SWRMC as a combined effort is better than the two commands running parallel-but-isolated lanes.

This is operationally adjacent to the broader BDR-relevant work — see §6.

## 4. SIMA San Diego stood up on Day 3 (2026-06-04, 1430 ceremony)

A separate Day-3 narrative from Robinson's keynote, with structural implications for SRF-JRMC's Pacific Fleet neighbor.

### 4.1 What SIMA is and who's driving it

SIMA = Shore Intermediate Maintenance Activity. The CNO issued a December 2025 mandate to NAVSEA to stand up a SIMA to help establish combat-ready forces. The chain of execution:

- **Vice Admiral James P. Downey** (NAVSEA Commander) tasked CNRMC.
- **Rear Admiral Daniel L. Lannaman** (CNRMC) directed SWRMC and MARMC to split off their Production Departments (Code 900) into separate SIMAs.

The same restructuring is happening at MARMC on the Atlantic side. Both SIMAs are being stood up under the same mandate.

## 5. Programs that touch SRF-JRMC's work (coatings, specs, training)

### 5.1 Standard Item 009-032 FY-28 Change 1 (Mark Ingle, NAVSEA 05P2)

Published 31 March 2026. Mark Ingle is the senior coatings technical authority — the Technical Warrant Holder — at NAVSEA 05P2, a sub-organization inside Mark Lattner's 05P. NAVSEA adopted 49 of 79 industry-proposed changes (about 60%).

**The top five changes:**

1. **A new zinc-rich paint option** (MIL-DTL-24441 Type III Formula 159) added to Table 2 Lines 1B/1C. 85%+ zinc by weight. Gives cathodic protection where the coating gets scratched. Four qualified suppliers.
2. **A new biocide-boosted foul-release antifouling** (MIL-PRF-24647E Type IV). Alternative to copper-based antifouling. Up to 7-year service life.
3. **A streamlined waterfront process for two-coat interior topcoats** (paragraph 3.1.16).
4. **A new magazine and ammunition hold coating note** (Note 12) — MIL-DTL-24441 Type IV Formula 152 white epoxy for magazines holding saluting charges.
5. **Fluidized-bed powder coating expanded** to airtight doors, watertight doors, hatches, and scuttles (Table 2 Line 41).

These are direct changes to surface-prep and coating work. Worth a focused review by SRF technical authority to find where Change 1 affects current SRF-Japan work.

### 5.2 MIL-PRF-16173F (Mark Ingle, NAVSEA 05P2)

New Class 8 "Persistent" preservatives added — for spaces where it's not practical to install a coating (rudder interiors, bilge keels, voids). Andy Vazquez (NAVSEA) showed an application in service; I didn't catch which ship or shipyard.

Publication held August 2025 to late May 2026 by a NAVAIR-vs-NAVSEA format dispute. NAVAIR objected to a new Type/Class/Grade format that would have forced expensive updates to downstream NAVAIR documents. The resolution kept the Class/Grade format and the first five coating identifiers similar to legacy 16173E. Resolved about one week before MegaRust. (This explains Matt Chu's Day-1 reference to "Mark, I knew, we had a huge conversation about that.")

Class 8 makes official a maintenance approach sailors have already been using in the field. SRF-JRMC may already have field experience with the underlying technique even if the Class 8 designation itself is new.

### 5.3 The Anti-Rust Program (Mark Lattner, NAVSEA 05P)

Post-launch as of January 2026. Two parallel tracks:

- **CCAT (Corrosion Control Assistance Teams)** — the established sailor-training-and-tools model, 16 years old, nine locations worldwide. SRF has a CCAT team out, which puts SRF-JRMC in the program footprint on the CCAT side.
- **SWARM Teams (Swift Warship Action on Rust Mitigation)** — contractor crews doing production preservation work during short availabilities. They attack running rust at deck edges and work surfaces sailors can't easily reach during deployments. Techniques include Paint Protective Film (PPF).

**Numbers since January 2026:** 85 ship availabilities worked, about 60,000 square feet of preservation done, more than 3,000 additively-manufactured parts delivered. Lattner: "more AM components on Navy ships than everyone else combined."

**SWARM Teams alongside sailors, not replacing them.** On Day 1, Andrew Sheets (Marine Corps) and Ian Shannon (Navy aviation) made a structural observation: once contractors take on preservation work, it becomes hard to give that work back to sailors later because sailors stop learning the trade. On Day 2, Lattner addressed this directly — the Anti-Rust Program structures SWARM Teams to work alongside sailors rather than take work over from them.

**SWARM Team geographic footprint.** Lattner didn't name the footprint in his brief. Whether SWARM Team coverage extends to SRF-Japan is a separate question and one I can put to the program POCs if you want.

**Industry Day:** 14-15 July in Norfolk VA. POC is Josh Chapman, NCMS, Josh.Chapman@ncms.org.

### 5.5 CAPT Richard Duldulao (NSWC Port Hueneme Chief Engineer, MIL) — combat-systems corrosion keynote

Duldulao opened his Day-2 keynote: "When a part of the [combat] system degrades, [the] kill chain potentially weakens. ... persistent, quiet, and relentless adversary. It doesn't sleep, doesn't need a supply chain, and operates on every single ship in the fleet 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. That adversary is the maritime environment, and its weapon of choice is, as we all know, corrosion."

**Cost data from his slides:**

- $14M to replace one SPY-1 array. Three arrays per DDG.
- $450K to replace one FCS MK 99 director, plus the alignment and collimation work that follows.
- $500K to resurface one AEM/S mask.

**Named hulls with FCS director corrosion replacements during recent availabilities:** USS Wayne E. Meyer (DDG-108) and USS Cole (DDG-67). Both Atlantic Fleet; Duldulao did not name any Pacific Fleet hulls.

**FY25 INSURV combat-systems data:** about 900 deficiencies attributed directly to corrosion, more than 660 of which actually degraded mission capability.

**On RAM.** "A few seized train bearings in the active fleet" — RAM launchers physically unable to slew. A multi-million-dollar launcher that can't track or engage an incoming threat.

**On AEM/S structural corrosion.** Severe localized corrosion in the crow's nest area that ship's force can't easily reach. The bottom band that carries the entire mast weight is severely corroded with interior surfaces inaccessible. Dissimilar-materials interfaces drive the corrosion. Tim Tenopir mentioned in passing that "some future LPDs don't have AEM/S anymore" — implying the AEM/S structure is being dropped from some upcoming LPD hulls.

**On Ship Refurbishment Program (OSRP) for NSSMS and RAM.** 30-year cost-savings figures from Duldulao's slide: $25.9M per CVN, $25.1M per LHA/LHD, $21.9M per LPD/LSD compared to the Depot Overhaul alternative. OSRP cycle is 3-6 weeks vs. Depot Overhaul's 1 year.

**NSWC PHD uses a different coating spec than NAVSEA 05P2.** In Q&A, Tim Tenopir (NSWC PHD, Office of Engineering and Technology) said NSWC PHD has moved off MIL-PRF-23236 and MIL-PRF-24635 polysiloxane on combat-systems composite structures (AEM/S, radar enclosures). They use MIL-PRF-85285 instead — an aviation-derived polyurethane spec, the standard topcoat on all U.S. Navy and Marine Corps aircraft per MIL-STD-2161. Mark Ingle's coatings work at NAVSEA 05P2 stays on the MIL-PRF-24635F polysiloxane and MIL-PRF-23236 epoxy track for HM&E (hull, mechanical, electrical) topside work. So there are two parallel coating specs in use now: combat systems on composites use MIL-PRF-85285; HM&E topside uses the 23236/24635 chemistry.

### 5.8 SWRMC's advanced manufacturing program (John Robinson's Day-3 keynote)

Robinson rejected "additive manufacturing" as the framing. His definition: **advanced manufacturing = CNC + AM + reverse engineering**. CNC is the mature capability; AM is being learned; **reverse engineering is the Achilles heel** because engineers need real tools (more than calipers and micrometers) to take a broken part with no drawings and produce CAD-ready drawings for CNC manufacturing.

The named machine is a **DMG MORI** laser-deposit additive manufacturing system, months into commissioning. Inside machinists are trying to develop weld processes — work Robinson said should be engineers'. Vice Admiral McLane (Naval Surface Forces) pushes Capt Kerosich (Robinson's CO at SWRMC) weekly on AM progress.

**Three example projects Robinson cited:**

1. **5-inch gate valves and larger** — supply system out of seats; throwing valves away was wasteful. SWRMC laser-deposits material onto the eroded seat and cuts it off with CNC.
2. **Shaft coupling bolts** — exotic, not readily available; sometimes the ship is docked and the bolts are missing. SWRMC reverse-engineered them, made plastic models, now producing experimental versions on CNC.
3. **Bridge crane motor on an amphib** — crane down for an extended period. OEM said the motor was replaced by a different model with different mounting hole pattern. Reverse engineers mapped existing and new motor footprints on-site and produced a "drill here, drill there" plan instead of building a new foundation.

Robinson's named reverse-engineering gap is a direct fit for Amanda Keach (SRF-JRMC AM Program Manager). See §11 for the action item.

## 7. Trip-wide patterns

These are observations across more than one session.

### 7.1 Combat-systems corrosion and HM&E topside corrosion are organized as separate domains

NSWC Carderock handles HM&E (hull, mechanical, electrical) topside corrosion — deck edges, hull plating, painted surfaces. The framework is Standard Item 009-032 and the MIL-PRF-23236 epoxy + MIL-PRF-24635 polysiloxane chemistry. Foley's composite-work-cell program lives here. Mark Ingle's coatings authority covers this domain. PCoE manages the spec updates.

NSWC Port Hueneme handles combat-systems corrosion — AEGIS, NSSMS, RAM, AEM/S, UNREP equipment. The funding pathway is different (In-Service Engineering Agent and the On Ship Refurbishment Program), the coating spec is different (MIL-PRF-85285 polyurethane), and the program structure is different.

For SRF-JRMC: if SRF-JRMC executes combat-systems composite-structure work on LPDs (AEM/S, radar enclosures, antenna structures), the coating spec is the MIL-PRF-85285 polyurethane track, not the 23236/24635 polysiloxane track. The two-spec split affects LPD work in Sasebo. See §11 for the action item.

### 7.2 Three different Navy organizations are working on hidden-corrosion non-destructive inspection (NDI)

- **Air Force drone-mapping** of C-5 and C-17 exterior corrosion with auto-generated maintenance action forms (Day-1 cross-service panel).
- **NSWC Port Hueneme patent-pending thermography and fiber-optics** non-destructive inspection (Duldulao's Day-2 keynote).
- **Edify magnetic-crawler with pulsed eddy current array (PECA) and ultrasonic, pierside or in a wet berth** (Apt's Day-2 robotics technical track pitch).

Same problem class — finding corrosion before you can see it — three different organizations, three different platforms. The Edify pierside / wet-berth approach is the one closest to SRF-JRMC's availability-cycle work: same hull inspection mission, deployable during a non-docking maintenance period to inform docking-availability scope planning.

### 7.3 Industry presentations at the Day-2 robotics technical track

Nate Livesey (Anti-Rust Program PM Acting) moderated four industry presentations on Day-2 afternoon. The lineup reads as an industry-evaluation lineup under the Anti-Rust Program. Possible subcontractor or teaming candidates if SRF leadership wants to surface any of these capabilities for an SRF-Japan availability:

- **Apellix (Jim Kunkle)** — spray-painting drones, semi-autonomous and autonomous. Third appearance at MegaRust 2026 (Day-1 NSRP pitch, Day-2 Lattner reference, Day-2 deep dive).
- **Edify (Jeff Apt)** — magnetic-crawler pierside / wet-berth NDI with pulsed eddy current array and ultrasonic. Apt cited a CBO report (publication 61940, "Maintenance Delays for Conventional Navy Ships," December 2025) on 14 years of yard-period overruns as the data foundation for the value pitch.
- **SwRI (George Samet, Intelligent Systems Division)** — robotics state of the art. Samet framed the field's current limit as a "data-to-action gap": robots collect data but don't assess, plan, execute, or report. SwRI is in R&D phase, not production.
- **PPG Protective and Marine Coatings (Jim McCarthy, Director Tech Ops US/Canada)** — electrostatic coating application. PPG is one of the suppliers under MIL-PRF-24635F. McCarthy was pinch-hitting for Bart Dima.

## 8. Broader policy context

### 8.1 NDAA §345 — Technology Enhancement for Surface Ship Maintenance

Public Law 119-60 §345, signed 18 December 2025. The Secretary of the Navy is required to investigate and adopt advanced surface-ship-maintenance technology. Drone-based inspection for uncrewed structural surveys is the specifically prioritized category. Adoption is mandatory. The section names no deadlines, dollar amounts, or specific programs.

For SRF-JRMC: §345 is the standing policy reason behind multiple program activities the trip documents — the Anti-Rust Program, NSWC Port Hueneme's NDI work, PCoE's equipment deployments to the maintenance centers, and the SIMA stand-ups. Surface-ship maintenance technology adoption at SRF-JRMC will increasingly run on the §345 mandate.

(Conlan Hsu (NSRP) cited §345 at the opening of the Day-1 NSRP SPC panel meeting. I had also brought it into the trip as pre-trip context.)

### 8.2 PAE acquisition reform (Vice Admiral Dave Lewis, ASNE President)

Lewis opened Day 2 with this framing: for 70 years, the Navy organizations that buy ships have not been the same organizations that have to maintain them later. That separation is why corrosion-prevention work has been chronically underfunded — the buyer has no incentive to pay for what only the maintainer pays for. The new Portfolio Acquisition Executive (PAE) construct puts the buyer and the maintainer under the same person, with contracting, engineering, and management all in one place.

Lewis told the audience: "Go find your PAE and make nice with that person — they now are the solution to our corrosion issues."

For SRF-JRMC: the PAE relevant to forward-deployed Pacific surface-ship maintenance is the new center of gravity for sustainment funding. Identifying that PAE is a structural question worth surfacing at SRF leadership level.

### 8.3 The DOD's annual cost-of-corrosion report stopped in 2020

Dr. Walter A. Juzukonis (AFLCMC) told the Day-1 cross-service panel that the LMI / Eric Hertzberg data pipeline that used to produce the figure is gone. Paul Chang (currently running the DOD Corrosion Policy Oversight Office) said the restart is "in the works." Matt Chu (NAVAIR) cited "$200 billion" for Naval aviation corrosion cost on Day 1 and qualified it himself: "I keep thinking it's made up."

For SRF-JRMC: any corrosion-cost figure in a current Navy or DOD brief is using pre-2020 data with no recent verification.

### 8.4 The "we pay for proof of concept and stop funding it" pattern came up five times

- An unidentified audience challenger on the Day-1 cross-service panel: depot-side corrosion findings don't reach Program Acquisition Executives.
- Dr. Maureen Foley on Day 1: "Most of our stuff is all back fit, mainly because we can't get into the new acquisition."
- Vice Admiral Lewis's Day-2 PAE keynote — the policy-level acknowledgment and the named fix.
- Tim Tenopir on Day 2: the "ten rolls of duct tape" laser-weapon prototype story — "You asked for proof of concept. You funded proof of concept. You didn't fund even prototype."
- John Robinson on Day 3: framing expeditionary maintenance as an unfunded mission that SWRMC is doing anyway because the fleet needs it.

Five independent people across Vice-Admiral to ISEA to senior-engineer to staff-scientist to ED level. The pattern is recognized broadly inside the Navy.
