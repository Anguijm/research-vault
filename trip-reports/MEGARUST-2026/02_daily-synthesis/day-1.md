## Day-1 executive takeaways

These are the items an executive (CACI or SRF-JRMC) needs to know from Day 1.

### 1. The DOD annual cost-of-corrosion report sunset in 2020 and the data pipeline is broken.

The LMI-produced annual cost-of-corrosion data (Eric Hertzberg, analyst) is no longer available. Paul Chang (current DOD Corrosion Policy Oversight Office director) told Walter (Dr. Walter A. Juzukonis, AFLCMC) the restart is "in the works" via a ROI investigation, but no current product exists. **Any future brief or briefing citing a Navy / DOD corrosion cost figure is citing pre-2020 data.** Matt Chu's reference to "$200 billion" in Naval aviation corrosion cost is a vintage figure — he himself flagged it ("I keep thinking it's made up"). [Source: clip 5, 44:35–46:55.]

### 2. ST-1 has Japan presence and is hiring.

The Surface Team One (ST-1) Corrosion COP — run by James Holman as PM, with Holman separately employed by SURFMEPP — was re-baselined with a new contract effective **2026-05-01**, **80 new hires** brought on November 2025, four new Knowledge Sharing Structures (KSS), and a **Pacific footprint in Hawaii, Japan, and San Diego**. First two ships are **DDG 2.0 mods (East Coast + West Coast)** — the operator's home ship class. Operator (almost certainly J. Anguiano) asked Holman who his Japan POC is; Holman offered to look it up from his shared drive (clip 6, 01:14:26–01:14:44). The Japan POC name is a known-pending follow-up item. [Source: clip 6 James Holman segment.]

### 3. PCoE-funded heated wire bend tables are being deployed to every RMC.

NSWC Carderock (Dr. Maureen Foley, ISEA — In-Service Engineering Agent — for smaller composite components and polymer AM) has 25+ years of composite/AM-ship-part portfolio targeted at DDGs and RMCs. The Paint Center of Excellence (PCoE) program has funded heated wire bend tables for deployment to "all our RMCs" — letting RMCs locally fabricate anti-siphon guards, composite gauge panels, and deck-edge scupper components on water-jet-cut sheet stock (~5 minutes per bend). Foley also has the **first publicly-released AM technical data packages** in the trip — three composite caps (receptacle, sound-powered phone box, IVCS box) plus two more. **SRF-JRMC is explicitly on the active deployment list** per Dr. Foley's "Composite Work Cell Concept" slide (operator photo evidence 2026-06-03): NSWCCD is currently working with MARMC, SWRMC, NWRMC, Rota, **SRF-Japan**, and Pearl Harbor. [Source: clip 6 Dr. Foley segment, 43:07–53:30, plus operator-provided slide image `bbf27411-2266.jpg`.]

### 4. Atmospheric plasma surface preparation is fully approved and deployed on Navy ships at sea.

In the clip-5 panel Q&A, "Dennis / Denny" (identity unconfirmed, but speaking with significant authority) confirmed atmospheric plasma blasting is "fully approved" as a Navy standard surface-preparation procedure and is being deployed on ships at sea, including **down to within 3 inches of the submarine pressure hull**. Passed all tech-warrant-holder requirements and the fatigue test. This is currently-deployed surface-prep tech that any SRF-JRMC brief on surface-prep options should account for. [Source: clip 5, 22:52–23:48.]

### 5. The acquisition-sustainment feedback gap is a recurring panel observation.

Two independent panels surfaced the same structural complaint: depot-side corrosion findings don't flow back to Program Acquisition Executives, so the next aircraft / ship lot doesn't get the corrosion-redesign benefit of sustainment-side learning. An unidentified CMB-aligned audience member challenged the clip-5 cross-service panel on this directly (01:54:23–01:56:39). Dr. Foley (clip 6, 58:00–59:25) independently expressed the same frustration from a composite-AM angle: "Most of our stuff is all back fit, mainly because we can't get into the new acquisition." Walter (clip 5) reported a partial Air Force fix — AFLCMC (3-star) now reports directly to SAF/AQ rather than through AFMC (4-star) — which may improve sustainment-to-acquisition feedback flow. Two independent surfacings of the same structural pattern make it a candidate for an exec-brief callout.

### 6. NDAA Sec 345 is the policy lens — and it changes how to read the rest of Day 1.

Public Law 119-60 §345 ("Technology Enhancement for Surface Ship Maintenance," enacted 2025-12-18) requires the Secretary of the Navy to investigate and adopt advanced technologies improving Navy surface ship maintenance, with **drone-based inspection for uncrewed structural surveys** specifically prioritized. The provision is mandatory but has no published deadlines, dollar amounts, or named-program callouts. Conlan Hsu (NSRP) cited §345 at the opening of the Day-1 SPC panel meeting (operator-direct observation; that opening portion was not recorded). Operator also surfaced this on 2026-06-03 as pre-trip policy context. **Re-read against this provision, several Day-1 items move from "interesting" to "directly responsive to a statutory mandate":**
- The clip-5 Air Force drone-based corrosion-mapping discussion (C-5 / C-17 exterior mapping with auto-generated maintenance action forms) is now the precedent the Navy must look at under §345.
- Dr. Foley's NSWC Carderock composite/AM portfolio (clip 6) is exactly the "advanced technology" category §345 covers.
- The ST-1 / SURFMEPP re-baselining + 80 new hires (clip 6) is plausibly downstream of the §345 mandate — operator should look for explicit §345 references in any SURFMEPP / ST-1 contract documentation.
- PCoE-funded heated wire bend tables to every RMC (clip 6) is also responsive to §345.

§345 is the policy frame for any Day-1 item that touches Navy surface-ship-maintenance technology adoption. [Source: operator-supplied 2026-06-03; verified via [Holland & Knight FY26 NDAA analysis](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/fy-2026-national-defense-authorization-act) and [S.1071, 119th Congress](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1071/text).]
