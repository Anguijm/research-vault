## Day-3 in one paragraph

Day 3 was a single Day-3 morning keynote from John Robinson, Executive Director of Southwest Regional Maintenance Center (SWRMC), framed as the announcement of SIMA San Diego standing up the same afternoon at Naval Station 32nd Street, Building 3116. Robinson walked through the CNO's December 2025 mandate driving NAVSEA (Vice Admiral James P. Downey) and CNRMC (Rear Admiral Daniel L. Lannaman) to split SWRMC's Production Department (Code 900) and MARMC's equivalent off into separate SIMAs — Shore Intermediate Maintenance Activities — under Capt Bill Albert at SIMA San Diego. Around 900 sailors moved to SIMA today. Robinson framed his program against a 2027-conflict-likely backdrop: tenders don't exist for the surface force, so SWRMC has pre-staged ~50 expeditionary maintenance containers ("shops in the box") across the Pacific for battle damage repair, working in co-responsibility with SRF-JRMC as the in-theater maintenance authority — Pat McDermott driving. Robinson rejected "additive manufacturing" framing in favor of "advanced manufacturing" (CNC + AM + reverse engineering), named reverse engineering as the Achilles heel of the program, and described a DMG MORI laser-deposit machine that was months into commissioning with inside machinists trying to figure out weld processes. The Q&A produced a direct on-tape interaction between Dr. Maureen Foley (NSWC Carderock, Day-1 clip 6 speaker) and Robinson — Foley introduced herself, Robinson immediately said "Yes, ma'am, you gave us a new machine this week," confirming the Composite Work Cell deployment to SWRMC happened during MegaRust week (and that the machine arrived missing an arm and hadn't been exercised yet).

## Day-3 executive takeaways

These are the items an executive (CACI or SRF-JRMC) needs to know from Day 3.

### 1. SIMA San Diego stood up 2026-06-04 at 1430

The Shore Intermediate Maintenance Activity (SIMA) is a structural restructuring under a December 2025 CNO mandate, executed through NAVSEA → CNRMC → SWRMC/MARMC. Approximately 900 sailors at SWRMC moved to SIMA today, with the exception of ~14 Engineering Duty Officers, one supply corps officer, and one SWO XO. **Captain Bill Albert** is the Commanding Officer. **Captain Carl Brandel** is the training department head. Full unraveling of the parent-organization separation runs through June 2029, governed by a 100-page MOA.

The four CIMA principles Admiral Lannaman gave Robinson:

1. Sailor self-sufficiency
2. Consolidate military personnel
3. Prioritize mission effectiveness
4. Identify and address gaps

Robinson's argument: SWRMC's Production Department has operated against these principles for 21 years already; SIMA is a restructuring, not a new build. (Source: clip 10.)

### 2. Direct SRF-Japan co-responsibility on the expeditionary maintenance container mission

Robinson on tape: SWRMC is "actually sending project teams for contract oversight, working with SRF Japan, which of course is the area of maintenance coming under out there to support these things." The program is the Pacific-theater **expeditionary maintenance container pre-staging mission** — approximately 50 containers ("shops in the box"), pre-positioned across the Pacific, activated by SWRMC sailors and civilians flown out from San Diego, capable of staying in place or being moved to an austere location. Currently single-ship 2-week deployments. **Pat McDermott** is driving the program. The mission is unfunded.

**Important framing correction per operator 2026-06-05:** Robinson's on-tape statement that SWRMC is "working with SRF Japan" describes co-responsibility, not SWRMC supporting SRF — SRF-JRMC owns substantial portions of this work in theater. Robinson and SRF-JRMC are both responsible for executing the mission.

Robinson cited the "Freon here isn't the same as Freon overseas, can't ship US Freon overseas" issue as one operational reality. Per operator (not on tape): there's also a calibration-gas problem — gases used to calibrate gas-free devices are "literally bad gas" and can't move by airfreight either. Broader category of items in these containers that can't ship via standard airfreight is a real planning constraint.

This is the most BDR-FLEET-READINESS-adjacent content in the trip. (See §"Cross-references to BDR-FLEET-READINESS opportunity" below.) (Source: clip 10.)

(Source: clip 10.)

### 4. Advanced manufacturing is CNC + AM + reverse engineering, with reverse engineering as the bottleneck

Robinson rejected "additive manufacturing" as the framing. His definition: **advanced manufacturing = CNC + AM + reverse engineering**. CNC is the mature capability; AM is being learned; **reverse engineering is the Achilles heel** because engineers need real tools (more than calipers and micrometers) to take a broken part with no drawings and produce CAD-ready drawings for CNC manufacturing.

The named machine is a **DMG MORI** laser-deposit additive manufacturing system, months into commissioning, with inside machinists trying to develop weld processes (which Robinson said should be engineers' work). Vice Admiral Brendan McLane (Naval Surface Forces) pressures Capt Kerosich (Robinson's CO at SWRMC) weekly on AM progress.

Three example projects Robinson cited:

1. **5-inch gate valves and larger** — laser-deposit material onto eroded seats, machine off with CNC. Replaces the throw-away-the-valve pattern in the supply chain.
2. **Shaft coupling bolts** — exotic, hard to find, sometimes the ship is docked and the bolts are unavailable. Reverse-engineered, plastic models made, CNC production underway.
3. **Bridge crane motor on an amphib** — OEM motor replaced by new model with different mounting hole pattern. Reverse engineers mapped existing and new motor footprints on-site and produced a drill-here-drill-there plan instead of building a new foundation.

**This is the direct hook for Amanda Keach (SRF-JRMC AM Program Manager) to engage with SWRMC at the ED level on reverse engineering** — operator's "when I get home" item 5 in `srf-full-report.md` §8.

(Source: clip 10.)

### 5. SWRMC organization context Robinson laid out

- SWRMC stood up in 2004 from five separate commands (a "shotgun wedding"). Robinson was on the original stand-up team.
- 2006: ordered military stand-down (not SWRMC's choice). Two leaders Robinson credited as future-looking through that period — **Bill Riswick** (deceased) and **Jim Ockenbach** (Robinson's predecessor as Executive Director, still alive).
- 2010: SWRMC moved off "crude Navy lean" to a value-focused operational model.
- 2026: second restructuring — SIMA San Diego splits off Code 900.

Robinson's framing: the organization has been "built for this" because Code 900 has been doing the four SIMA-principle activities for 21 years. SIMA is structural reorganization of an extant capability, not a new build.

### 6. The four RMC missions Robinson named, and what's moving to SIMA

SWRMC has four funded missions:

1. **Contract Management Oversight (CMO)** — depot-level contract teams.
2. **Assessment** — Code 200 engineering department, on-ship assessments between CNO Availabilities.
3. **Fleet Technical Assistance (FTA)** — on-board and distance support, including the Souda Bay CIWS-fix anecdote.
4. **Intermediate Maintenance** — "straight stick" maintenance work.

**Mission 4 (Intermediate Maintenance) moves to SIMA San Diego.** **Expeditionary Maintenance** (the unfunded Pacific shops-in-a-box program — §2 above) also moves to SIMA. The other missions stay with SWRMC.
