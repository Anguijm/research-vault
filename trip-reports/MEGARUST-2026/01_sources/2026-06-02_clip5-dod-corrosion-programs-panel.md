# MegaRust 2026 — DOD Corrosion Programs Panel (afternoon plenary)

## Summary

This is the 2-hour afternoon plenary, which combined the conference opening administrative session with the Department of Defense Corrosion Programs Panel — a cross-service panel that pulled corrosion-program representatives from Navy aviation, the Marine Corps (ground), the Air Force, and the Army into one room. The session was moderated by Lauren (Deputy Department of Navy Corrosion Control and Prevention Executive). It is the first conference event ever held in this newly-built Marriott Mission Valley plenary room (the hotel itself was on its opening day, per Dave Zilber).

The panel was structured around four pillars: strategic overview and funding; field realities of surface preparation and rapid repair; the human element (training, retention, leadership culture); and industry-and-innovation engagement. Most of the substantive content is **anecdotal organizational-and-policy material**, not technical specification material. The field-realities and industry-engagement pillars contain the material with cleanest CACI / SRF-JRMC adjacency — deployed maintenance models the panelists describe (Marine Corps mobile service containers in Darwin Australia and Ohio; Air Force drone-mapping for corrosion; cold-spray-with-3D-printed-mobile-shell repairs) parallel forward-deployed Navy ship-maintenance work SRF-JRMC performs.

A policy fact worth flagging: **the annual DOD cost-of-corrosion report sunset in 2020**. Dr. Walter A. Juzukonis (the Air Force panelist; operator-confirmed 2026-06-03) confirmed that the LMI-produced annual cost-of-corrosion data is no longer available, that Paul Chang (current Corrosion Policy Oversight Office director) is "in the works" on restarting it, and that the prior data was the way any service quantified weapon-system corrosion cost. This is a structural information gap that affects how the operator should weight any "cost of corrosion" claim in a future brief.

**Speaker-attribution note (resolved 2026-06-03).** The Air Force panelist is **Dr. Walter A. Juzukonis** (AFLCMC; operator-confirmed). Whisper rendered the introduction as "Dr. Juan Pachacones" — significantly garbled. Throughout the panel he is correctly referred to as "Walter" by other panelists. **The Navy aviation panelist is Matt Chu (NAVAIR, FRC Southwest); operator-confirmed. Whisper rendered Matt Chu's introduction as "Matthew Stewart" — also garbled.** Matt Chu had to leave partway through the panel; his teammate Ian Shannon (corrosion analyst, Combat Air Pack / TYCOM corrosion CCC team) stepped in from the audience to cover the back half of the panel for Naval aviation, including the "we call it crack" workforce-civilian-ratchet quote and the post-panel cold-spray / 3D-printed-mobile-shell discussion.

### Panelist 2 — Andrew Sheets (Marine Corps, NSWC Carderock)

- **Position.** Marine Corps corrosion and coatings technical expert at NSWC Carderock Division (NSWCCD). Chief engineer of the Marine Corps Corrosion Prevention and Policy Act program. Tied to PMA-894 (Marine Corps vehicle and weapon system program). 30+ years in role.

- **Marine Corps program structure.** Centralized program centrally managed through Marine Corps Systems Command PA Marine Corps. Multiple pillars: assessments, RDT&E, corrosion repair. Cradle-to-grave from acquisition through operation/sustainment. Database of approximately 110,000–120,000 active corrosion records for Marine Corps vehicles, integrated into the GCSS Marine Corps system.

- **Mobile Service Container (deployed corrosion control).** Currently deployed with Marine Corps fleet forces in Darwin, Australia, and with an operating attachment in Ohio. Expanding. Used because forward-deployed gear stays at deployed locations (does not get packed and brought back for repair) — corrosion control must travel forward with the gear.

- **Aviation-ground cross-pollination.** Sheets noted Marine Corps is looking at sharing depot-level assets across Army / Navy / Marine Corps aviation — "new age thinking in the world of, these are my toys, I'm not sharing my space."

- **Three-tier zinc-rich coating pioneered ~20 years.** Sheets cited a Marine-Corps-led zinc-rich coating system as a win — defying the conventional wisdom that zinc shouldn't be used on armor or high-strength steel in submerged environments, on the argument that a submerged truck is a worse problem than the coating choice. Result: a coating system that runs 10 years vs. the standard 3-year coating job.

- **MOS 0411 drawdown.** The Marine Corps' maintenance management MOS (0411) has been drawn down significantly. 0411s used to be in every sub-command of the MSCs (Major Subordinate Commands); now they're at the MSC level only. This is a structural workforce-readiness concern Sheets flagged.

- **Water bowls (1970s legacy gear) example.** Marine Corps still uses water bowls built in the 1970s with no replacement program. They rot from inside out (tube-frame corrosion). The Marine Corps clean engagement conference recently spent 1.5 days on water-bowl problems.

- **Industry ask.** Corrosion-prevention solution for electronics-in-water-collection environments inside ground vehicles — small electronic units in vehicles that weren't designed to take them, with chronic moisture-collection corrosion problems.

### Panelist 3 — Air Force (Walter / "Dr. Juan Pachacones")

Corrosion-program asks of $20–$25M typically yield $10–$15M in primary allocation, with year-end follow-on money when RSO can't spend its full allocation.

- **SBIR and CERDA PSTCP funding.** Air Force also funds corrosion R&D via Small Business Innovation Research (SBIR) and through the speaker's position on the CERDA PSTCP technical committee. Recent CERDA PSTCP statement of need: a chromic-acid-free pre-penetrant etchant for NDI work. Statements of need are normally due in July but were delayed by the 2025 government shutdown.

- **ACIP — Aircraft Structural Integrity Program.** Air Force's most effective corrosion-program structure. Led by Chuck Babish. ACIP visits every weapon-system program office annually. Asks corrosion-specific questions: does the weapon system have an assigned corrosion manager, has the manager completed required training, does the weapon system have a corrosion prevention control plan, and are corrosion advisory boards being run correctly. Reference docs: AFI 63-140, AFI/AFMC Supplement 63-140, MIL-STD-1530D.

LMI used to produce the data under Eric Hertzberg (analyst); Robert Kerwin then Paul Chang have been the Corrosion Policy Oversight Office (CPO) leads. After LMI funding was cut, Hertzberg's email-on-request access was the bridge for individual program offices for a few years, then that ended too. Paul Chang told Walter the restart is "in the works" via a company doing a return-on-investment investigation.

- **Best-investment-for-corrosion-dollars (historic).** When Walter previously asked Eric Hertzberg the best ROI for an Air Force corrosion investment, the answer was: landing gear for cargo aircraft.

- **Drone-based aircraft corrosion mapping.** Air Force is using drones to map the exterior of C-5s and C-17s, identify corrosion, and automatically generate maintenance action forms. Sensors on the drone measure paint and metal thickness to prioritize work. Civilian industry is doing similar.

- **F-16 laser outer-mold-line mapping.** Air Force uses a laser to map the entire F-16 outer mold line before stripping; the same map is recaptured next visit so changes can be measured against the prior baseline. Tunable to top-coat-only, down-to-primer, etc.

- **Additive remanufacturing.** Walter cited the Meltio (Whisper: "Miltio") additive-remanufacturing system inside CNC machines for repair-and-remachining of damaged parts including magnesium housing covers (E-2 and other aircraft).

- **Rapid Casting Program Center of Excellence (RCPCOE).** A company partnered with Air Force on 3D-printed casting molds for foundries. Foundries don't want to design casting molds for low-volume repair parts; RCPCOE bridges this with iterative 3D-printed mold development. Also makes the filters that prevent corrosion artifacts during magnesium pouring. A California magnesium foundry burned down ~2 months prior to MegaRust 2026; magnesium for military use is a critical and increasingly scarce resource.

- **B-1 bomber digital twin.** Two retired B-1 bombers were disassembled and 3D-scanned to create a digital twin. The twin is used with cold-spray to rebuild non-round fastener holes in B-1 panels for reuse, and to apply technical coatings on hydraulic lines.

- **Networked CNC machine fleet.** Air Force is standardizing CNC machines across Manufacturing Technology Offices so a repair developed in New Orleans can be executed at Alaska. Eliminates per-site reverse-engineering. Training portable across locations.

- **Surface Analyst.** Industry product Walter cited for measuring water contact angle on aircraft surfaces — replaces subjective "water break" test. Threshold: contact angle <50° = surface is clean enough for painting. Adoption-resistance pattern: technicians push back ("I would have to do that a million times on a big aircraft"). Lockheed Martin Fort Worth uses it on the F-35 line for nut-plate prep. Calibration discipline matters (Walter watched an LM technician fail two calibrations by putting his thumb on the calibration spot and then dropping the next sheet face-down on the floor).

- **Industry asks.** (1) NDI technique that can survey a large aircraft surface quickly while walking it ("Holy Grail" framing). (2) A polysulfide flexible primer replacement without chromium and with low VOC — current restrictions on the chromium version severely limit application.
