# MegaRust 2026 — Session 1: Additive Manufacturing and Composites

## Summary

This is the Day-1 afternoon technical session running parallel to the corrosion-policy track that Dave Zilber outlined at the end of clip 5. Three speakers presented over roughly 85 minutes:

1. **Dr. Greg Sweet** — Defense Research and Development Canada (DRDC), Naval Sustainment group, co-located with the Royal Canadian Navy East Coast Naval Base at CFB Halifax. Presented research comparing the corrosion behavior of nickel-aluminum-bronze (NAB / "NAP") produced via four additive-manufacturing techniques (laser powder bed fusion; laser blown powder directed energy deposition; wire-arc additive manufacturing / WAAM; cold spray) against conventional cast and wrought baselines. Tested in flowing natural seawater using both medium-term instrumented tank tests (~4 months) and long-term immersion on a DRDC test barge in Halifax Harbor.

2. **Dr. Maureen Foley** — NSWC Carderock, ISEA (In-Service Engineering Agent) for smaller composite components and polymer additive manufacturing. 25+ years at NSWC Carderock developing a portfolio of composite and 3D-printed parts for U.S. Navy surface ships. Walked through the breadth of her portfolio with strong DDG-class and Regional Maintenance Center (RMC) relevance.

3. **James Holman** — holds two roles: (a) **PM for the ST-1 (Surface Team One) Corrosion Community of Practice**; (b) **separately employed by SURFMEPP** (Surface Maintenance Engineering Planning Program). Operator-confirmed 2026-06-03 that ST-1 and SURFMEPP are distinct organizations; James Holman has effectively two jobs across both. Whisper originally rendered SURFMEPP as "CERF". Wrap-up of the morning ST-1 Corrosion COP meeting. Career background: Navy EMC retired 22 years + 15 years shipyard industry + 12 years at SURFMEPP. Walked through the ST-1 reorganization that took effect May 1, 2026 — including 80 new hires, four new Knowledge Sharing Structures (KSS), and a Pacific-theater footprint (Hawaii, Japan, San Diego).

**Two threads in the clip have direct SRF-JRMC adjacency** — Dr. Foley's RMC-deployable composite-and-AM portfolio (heated wire bend tables, sheet stock for anti-siphon guards, deck-edge scuppers, gauge panels, overboard discharge covers — all on DDGs) and James Holman's ST-1 reorganization (freshly re-baselined Surface Team One Corrosion COP with Pacific-theater presence including Japan, new contract effective May 1, 2026, 80 new hires). Whether either rises to actionable BD signal is the operator's call.

### Speaker 1 — Dr. Greg Sweet (DRDC Canada)

- **Affiliation.** Defense Research and Development Canada (DRDC), Naval Sustainment group. Civilian scientific advisory side of Canada's Department of National Defense. Co-located with the East Coast Naval Base at CFB Halifax, Nova Scotia.

- **Research focus.** Long-term seawater corrosion behavior of nickel-aluminum-bronze (NAB) produced via four AM methods (laser powder bed fusion; laser blown powder DED; WAAM; cold spray) vs. conventional cast and wrought baselines.

- **Why NAB.** "Workhorse" naval material used in valves, pumps, propellers across all global vessels. Generally good corrosion (0.025–0.05 mm/yr) but has an Achilles heel: **selective phase corrosion** can drive local rates to 0.5–1 mm/yr in some environments and component geometries. (Dr. Greg Sweet.)

- **Test materials.** Cast (UNS-C95800) and wrought (UNS-C63020) baselines + same wrought-grade material atomized by a university partner (InterCAST?) for the powder-bed and DED feedstock. WAAM uses off-the-shelf wire (chemistry close but not identical). Cold spray feedstock falls within UNS-C63020 chemistry but isn't an exact UNS grade.

- **Heat-treat caveat from the AM samples.** All AM samples followed the cast standard heat treatment. Q&A surfaced the point that this may be tradition rather than optimization — AM microstructure is fundamentally different from cast, so the cast heat treatment may not be the right one for AM. (Audience Q-and-A around 28:00.)

- **Test methodology.** Three stages: (1) short-term potentiodynamic testing for kinetic parameters; (2) medium-term — instrumented tanks with continuously flowing natural seawater piped in from the adjacent naval bay, ~4 months continuous immersion; (3) long-term — non-instrumented coupons on a DRDC test barge inside Halifax Harbor, exposed to seasonal cycles and marine fouling for many years.

- **Driving operational scenario.** The motivation for fully-immersed testing is real: certain valves on certain Canadian vessels stay wet for ~12 years continuous. (Dr. Greg Sweet, Q&A response.)

- **Tafel-slope assumptions.** Once kinetics were characterized, the project locked in anodic Tafel slope = 90 mV/decade and cathodic = 200 mV/decade for the medium-term work, allowing comparison across AM processes on consistent assumptions. (Dr. Greg Sweet.)

### Speaker 2 — Dr. Maureen Foley (NSWC Carderock)

- **3D-printed overboard discharge covers (DDG 51 class).** Sizes 2"–10" diameter, 2" and 4" lip lengths (DDG only needs up to 4"). Two material variants: flexible (impact-tolerant) and rigid. - **Sun shields — NAVSEA P4 requirement (bright white) and CVN-70 gray-vs-white demo.** NAVSEA P4 currently mandates bright white sun shields. (Dr. Foley.)

LPD-19 has a Larmack Foot in service for several years; a Surface Warfare Center stakeholder is interested in qualifying a related 3D-printed valve part (Whisper rendered the hull/program reference as "B-17 / strike force" but the actual hull/program identity is unverified and operator-confirmed not to be B-17; safely ignored).
