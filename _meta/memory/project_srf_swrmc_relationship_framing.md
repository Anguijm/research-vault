When describing SRF-JRMC's relationship to SWRMC (Southwest Regional Maintenance Center, San Diego — the operator's Pacific Fleet peer organization on the CONUS side), use this framing:

- **Peer commands with different AORs.** SRF-JRMC and SWRMC are peer Navy maintenance organizations with different geographic Areas of Responsibility. Neither command directs the other.
- **No formal teaming agreement currently in place.** The two commands do not have a formalized teaming arrangement today. That doesn't mean one couldn't exist in the future — the operator views teaming as valuable and doesn't want to rule it out.
- **Knowledge-sharing is a natural starting mode.** When SWRMC is further along on something (advanced manufacturing methodology, BDR-relevant expeditionary maintenance container science, etc.), SRF can learn from their work to avoid duplicating effort. This is how peer commands cooperate by default.
- **Each command owns its own AOR work.** SRF-JRMC owns substantial in-theater execution; SWRMC owns CONUS-side and the project-team contract oversight side. Co-responsibility on shared missions, with each command operating in its own lane.

**Why:** Operator (J. Anguiano, SRF-JRMC) corrected this framing on 2026-06-05 after I had over-stated the negative case ("NOT a teaming arrangement"):

> "I think the two commands would team. One command does not direct the other one, but there is no formal agreement for teaming. They are peers. They have different AORs, so I don't know. I don't like to say that they wouldn't team. I think teaming is important, but there is not currently a teaming agreement between them."

The earlier operator statement that "I'm not trying to team with Swarmic" should be read narrowly: not "teaming is ruled out" but "the immediate goal here isn't to set up a teaming arrangement — it's to learn what SWRMC has figured out." The longer-term posture is open.

**How to apply:**

1. **Don't make negative claims about teaming** in customer-facing artifacts. Don't write "not a teaming arrangement" or "SRF doesn't team with SWRMC." Both could be wrong in the future, and both put a negative cast on a relationship the operator values.

2. **Describe the immediate work** without ruling out the long-term shape of the relationship. "Connect Amanda Keach with SWRMC for knowledge sharing on advanced manufacturing" is enough — don't add "not a teaming arrangement" as a qualifier.

3. **When the operator says they want to avoid duplicating SWRMC's work,** read that as "the immediate value is knowledge transfer," not "we'll never team."

4. **Peer commands cooperate.** That's the default Navy framing. Knowledge sharing is one mode of cooperation; teaming on specific work is another; co-responsibility on shared missions is a third. All are legitimate. Don't oversimplify the relationship into one of them.

5. **Capture relationships in the report by what's happening now,** not by speculating about future organizational forms. Today: peer commands, knowledge sharing on advanced manufacturing and expeditionary maintenance containers, co-responsibility on the Pacific container mission. Tomorrow: open.
