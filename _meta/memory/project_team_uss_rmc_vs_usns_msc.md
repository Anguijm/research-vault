The operator's team works at a **Regional Maintenance Center in Japan
(SRF-JRMC)** and supports the Navy in **USS** ship repair — commissioned
warships, Navy-crewed. This is the team's direct-execute lane.

Do NOT conflate this with the **USNS** world that the `HANWHAOCEAN-DOD` research
covers. The Hanwha Ocean / Korea MRO work is all **USNS** — Military Sealift
Command (MSC) auxiliaries (USNS Wally Schirra, Yukon, Charles Drew = T-AKE / oiler
support ships) — contracted through **NAVSUP FLC Yokosuka**, run operationally by
MSC / MSCO Korea. Different ship category, different command, different
contracting shop than the operator's RMC/USS world. (The MSC FLCY industry-day
deck even notes USNS platforms no longer require an MSRA/ABR, while **USS**
platforms still do.)

**Why it matters for opportunity framing:** the USNS/Hanwha opportunity is
CACI-*corporate's* MSC lane (the operator can surface it as a relationship-lead,
but it is not the team's direct-execute work). The team's direct-execute lane is
**USS repair at the RMC**, which aligns better with CACI's NAVSEA / SeaPort-e /
SeaPort NxG past performance (ship design, NAVSEA SETA, naval forces logistics)
than with the MSC maintenance-engineering line. Open question that decides whether
the two worlds ever intersect: does forward-deployed **USS** warship depot work in
Japan/Pacific ever go to Korean/foreign yards, or stay at the RMC and U.S.-cleared
facilities?

**Pinned 2026-06-07 (operator-confirmed):** the operator's specific task order is
**FA807522F0054** — the MSC **N7 Engineering Directorate** task order ("Design,
Maintenance, Quality, Extension Engineering, and Shipyard Quality Assurance"),
held under the DTIC IAC MAC (FA807518D0006), administered by the Air Force 774
ESS, funded by MSC. That one task order spans at least two non-overlapping
contingents: (a) the operator's team — **USS** repair support at **SRF-JRMC,
Japan**; and (b) a separate **worldwide USNS shipyard-QA / maintenance-engineering
contingent** that deploys to MSC ship availabilities (the Hanwha-relevant team).
The operator has met some of the USNS contingent in San Diego; the two do not
operationally overlap. How an MSC-funded TO also covers USS/SRF-JRMC work is an
unresolved contracting detail (operator confirms the facts, not the mechanism).
This closes the capability-book gap "identify the operator-team's specific task
order and its sponsor." Implication for the Hanwha track: the USNS contingent on
the operator's *own* TO is the natural CACI provider of government-side QA at the
Korea/Hanwha USNS availabilities — so the operator's bridge is *internal and warm*
(same PIID), not a cold corporate intro.
