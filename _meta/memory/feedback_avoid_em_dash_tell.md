On 2026-06-13 the operator flagged that Claude overuses the em-dash ("—"). It is
grammatically valid, but leaning on it as the default connector is a recognizable
machine-writing pattern. "Practice like you play": this applies to every document,
internal research files included, not just customer-facing deliverables. (The operator
does not need existing docs retroactively scrubbed. Just write properly going forward.)

**Why:** prose that reads as AI-generated undercuts credibility. Keeping the habit out of
internal docs too keeps the writing consistent and stops the tell from leaking into
anything that ships.

**How to replace an em-dash (operator's house guide):**
1. Commas, for non-essential information that flows with the sentence. Example: "The
   primary objective, reducing shipyard turnaround times, requires strict scheduling."
2. Parentheses, for de-emphasized background, acronym definitions, or a skippable side
   note. Example: "The new protocol (developed by the logistics division) launches next
   quarter."
3. A colon, when introducing an explanation, conclusion, or list at the end of a
   sentence. Example: "The audit revealed the bottleneck: insufficient drydock capacity."
4. Restructure into separate sentences when the inserted thought is long or introduces a
   complex secondary idea.

Reserve the em-dash for rare, genuine emphasis; default to one of the four moves above.

overwrite an existing one) and [[feedback_readability]].
