# No unsupported superlatives — stop ranking facts

Stop writing hierarchical claims ("the most X," "the single most Y," "the highest-leverage Z," "the strongest case for," "the most directly relevant," "the most quotable") without a defensible basis for the ranking.

**Why:** Operator-pointed out 2026-06-04 that I had been freely tossing around superlatives across the MegaRust Day-1 outputs — "the single most CACI/SRF-JRMC-direct signal," "the most consequential observation," "the most-valuable analytical content," "the highest-leverage follow-up," etc. The operator's response: "How could you possibly know that?" Correct critique. I don't have visibility into:
- The operator's actual SRF-JRMC priorities and what is new-to-them vs. already-known
- CACI's BD portfolio context and where any given fact falls in their internal pipeline
- The downstream impact of any given fact on any decision the operator might make
- The relative weight any of these facts carry in real engagement decisions

Ranking facts is an editorial act that imports authority I don't have. It also undermines the operator's own evaluative work — if I pre-rank everything, the operator has to push back against my framing instead of doing the ranking themselves.

**How to apply:**

1. **Describe, don't rank.** Replace "the most operationally significant fact in the clip" with a description of what the fact is and what it touches. Let the operator weigh it.

2. **If comparison is genuinely useful, name the basis explicitly.** "Among the items in this clip, only this one X" or "this is the only Day-1 finding that combines criterion A with criterion B" is defensible. "The single most important" is not.

3. **Strip these patterns when reviewing drafts:** "the single most," "the most X-relevant," "highest-leverage," "the strongest case," "most directly relevant," "the most valuable," "the most quotable," "the most consequential," "the most operationally significant." Each one is a flag.

4. **Trip-reports and capture briefs are especially prone to this** because the genre invites editorializing. The discipline matters most in those artifacts. The FACT / Assessment / Speculation labeling in the vault SOP exists partly to keep my ranking impulses inside the Assessment lane where they belong — and even in Assessment, ranking needs a stated basis.

5. **The reader is doing their own ranking.** A trip-report or synthesis is most useful when it presents the facts and obvious structural patterns clearly, not when it tries to do the operator's job of weighing those facts for their own portfolio decisions.

This rule is binding on all trip-report content, capture briefs, exec summaries, daily syntheses, and any other prose-style artifact where I'm tempted to insert hierarchy. It applies to chat responses too — don't tell the operator that something is "the most" of anything unless I can defend the ranking on stated criteria.
