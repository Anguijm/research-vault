# "I don't know X" from operator does not mean "close X"

When the operator responds to an open question or follow-up item with "I don't know" or "I don't know what we're looking for," do NOT default to closing the item.

**Why:** On 2026-06-04 I had five open follow-up items on the Day-2 clip 7 source file. Operator said: "I don't know what we're lookin for for 1-4. Not attending industry day." I closed all five by interpreting "I don't know" as "skip these." Operator corrected: "I did not intend for you to close them." Correct interpretation was "your asks aren't clear enough — explain better."

The pattern: "I don't know X" from the operator can mean any of:
1. "I don't have the answer" → close the item or mark it as needing external resolution
2. "I don't know what you're asking" → re-frame the question more concretely
3. "I don't know why this matters" → state the operational stake explicitly
4. "I don't know whether to chase this" → give the operator the inputs they need to decide (what it is, why it might matter, when it would matter)

I defaulted to #1 when the operator meant #2/#3/#4. Cost: lost the open items and had to reverse the closures.

**How to apply:**

1. **Don't close on "I don't know" alone.** Ask back if the framing is unclear, or re-state the open item with three explicit components: (a) what specifically I'm asking, (b) why it might matter to the operator's work, (c) a low-priority flag if the underlying scenario isn't current.

2. **If "Not attending [event]" or other unambiguous decision is included with "I don't know," close only the unambiguous-decision item.** Don't extend the closure to the other items.

3. **When in doubt, ask one clarifying question rather than acting on the ambiguous read.** Cost of a clarifying question: one short turn. Cost of closing in error: lost items and a reversal.

4. **This applies to any context — not just trip-reports.** Open questions, decision items, follow-ups, capture-brief gates — the same rule. Operator's "I don't know" is information about their state, not necessarily a directive to act.
